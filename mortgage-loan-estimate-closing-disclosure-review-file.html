Mortgage Disclosure Review File: Loan Estimate, Closing Costs and Cash to Close

A mortgage closing is not the moment to trust that two similar totals mean the same deal. Put the selected Loan Estimate beside the Closing Disclosure, trace every changed term and fee, verify the cash movement through known contacts, and keep the file open until the documents and explanations agree.

Published November 11, 2026. General U.S. consumer organization, not legal, financial, tax, real-estate, title, insurance or lending advice. Mortgage forms, timing, tolerance rules and rights depend on the transaction and loan. Use the current documents and qualified lender, settlement, housing-counseling or legal help for the specific closing.

The useful answer

  • Use the most recent Loan Estimate for the loan actually selected; a quote, worksheet or earlier estimate is not the comparison record.
  • CFPB says borrowers generally receive the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing; use that window to resolve differences rather than merely acknowledge delivery.
  • Compare loan amount, rate, product, payment, prepayment penalty, balloon feature, closing costs and cash to close separately instead of checking only the bottom line.
  • Verify wire or cashier's-check instructions through a known, independently obtained contact; a plausible email is not verification.
  • Do not sign a document you do not understand. Record the discrepancy, owner, explanation, corrected version and closure evidence.

Open one controlled closing file

Create a file for the exact property, borrower group, lender and loan. Save every Loan Estimate with its issue date, the rate-lock record, intent-to-proceed confirmation, revised estimates, the Closing Disclosure, settlement contact list and any written explanations. Mark versions as superseded without deleting them. A screenshot of one portal screen does not replace the complete PDF and its date.

Write the scheduled closing date, delivery date and method for the Closing Disclosure, rate-lock expiration, financing contingency, inspection or appraisal deadlines and the person responsible for each answer. CFPB advises contacting the lender or closing agent at least a week before closing to learn how the disclosure will arrive and requesting other important closing documents in advance. The file should make time pressure visible before it becomes a reason to stop reading.

  • Property, borrower, lender and loan officer: ________
  • Selected Loan Estimate date and rate-lock status: ________
  • Closing Disclosure received / scheduled closing: ________
  • Settlement agent and independently verified contact: ________

Lock the intended loan terms before comparing fees

Copy the loan amount, term, purpose, product, loan type, interest rate and rate-lock status from the selected Loan Estimate. Record whether the rate is fixed or adjustable, whether the balance can grow despite on-time payments, and whether the form shows a prepayment penalty or balloon payment. CFPB identifies these as core details and flags penalties and balloon payments as risky features that deserve direct questions.

Then compare the same fields on the Closing Disclosure. A changed monthly payment can come from the loan terms, mortgage insurance, taxes, insurance or escrow assumptions, so identify which component moved. Do not accept 'the payment is about the same' when the rate, term, product or future adjustment changed. Ask for the reason, governing document and corrected disclosure when the record is wrong.

  • Loan amount / term / product / type: ________
  • Rate and lock expiration: ________
  • Principal and interest / mortgage insurance / escrow: ________
  • Penalty, balloon or adjustable feature: yes / no / question

Build a fee-by-fee variance ledger

List the Loan Costs and Other Costs using the form's own categories: origination charges, services the borrower could not shop for, services the borrower could shop for, taxes and government fees, prepaids, initial escrow payment and other charges. Put the Loan Estimate amount, Closing Disclosure amount, difference, party paid, seller or lender credit and explanation on one row. The point is not to assume every change is illegal; it is to make every change answerable.

CFPB notes that some fees cannot increase, some have a combined tolerance and some can change when permitted circumstances occur. Do not apply a remembered percentage to the whole form. Ask the lender which rule and circumstance applies to the exact line, and request the written calculation or corrected document. Compare the total origination charges and the services you could not shop for across offers because the lender controls those choices.

  • Fee and form section: ________
  • Estimate / final / difference: ________
  • Credit, adjustment or party paid: ________
  • Explanation, rule cited and corrected version: ________

Reconstruct cash to close from its parts

Cash to close is not simply the down payment plus a fee total. Trace the down payment, deposit already paid, loan amount, closing costs, seller credits, lender credits, prorations, adjustments and other funds shown on the forms. Match deposits to receipts and confirm whether a credit changed the interest rate or other terms. If the number is unexpected, CFPB says to ask the lender to explain why.

Record the exact approved payment method and source-of-funds documentation requested by the lender. Do not move money because of a last-minute email, text or caller. Confirm changed instructions using a phone number obtained earlier from a trusted document or in-person contact, not the message that announced the change. If money has already been misdirected, contact the bank or wire company immediately and use current law-enforcement reporting routes.

  • Down payment and deposit credit: ________
  • Closing costs, seller and lender credits: ________
  • Prorations and other adjustments: ________
  • Final cash to close and verified payment route: ________

Read the rest of the closing packet as a system

Ask for the promissory note, mortgage or deed of trust, deed, initial escrow disclosure and other transaction documents in advance when available. The note is the repayment promise; the security instrument gives the lender rights in the property if the loan is not repaid; the deed transfers ownership in a purchase. Names, property description, loan amount, payment terms and obligations should not contradict the Closing Disclosure.

Do not assume a familiar page title proves the content. Check the borrower and property names, signing capacity, dates, payment destination, late-charge terms, escrow treatment, occupancy statements and attached riders. A refinance may have a right-to-cancel notice under applicable rules; a purchase usually does not create the same general federal rescission period. Ask qualified counsel about rights instead of treating a website checklist as a legal conclusion.

  • Promissory note compared: yes / no / issue
  • Mortgage or deed of trust and riders compared: ________
  • Deed and vesting names reviewed with qualified help: ________
  • Escrow, insurance, taxes and first-payment route: ________

Run every discrepancy through one closure record

For each issue, quote the field, amount or wording; name the document and page; state what you expected; identify the person who owns the answer; and record the promised correction or explanation. Save the corrected document as a new version. A verbal assurance that 'it will be fixed after closing' is not the same as a corrected obligation, posted credit or written settlement instruction.

Classify the result as corrected, explained and accepted, escalated, referred for housing counseling or legal advice, or unresolved. CFPB says to get errors fixed before closing and not to sign papers you do not understand. That may affect timing or the transaction, so coordinate promptly with the lender, settlement professional, real-estate professional and qualified attorney rather than improvising a legal remedy.

  • Document / page / exact discrepancy: ________
  • Owner, contact date and reference number: ________
  • Written explanation or corrected version: ________
  • Closed / escalated / unresolved and decision: ________

Close the file after funding and recording, not at the table

After signing, save the complete executed packet, final Closing Disclosure, proof of funds, receipts and contact information. Confirm where and when the first payment is due using verified servicing information. Watch for a legitimate servicing transfer notice and do not redirect payments based on an unsolicited message. Store sensitive documents securely; mortgage and property data can also attract targeted solicitations after recording.

Compare the final bank movement with the approved cash-to-close record and confirm any promised refund, credit or corrected document. Note when the deed and mortgage are expected to record and how official copies are obtained in the jurisdiction. Close only when the executed terms, funds, ownership record and follow-up obligations are accounted for. Retention requirements vary, so get tax or legal advice before destroying original closing records.

  • Executed packet and final disclosure saved: ________
  • Funds matched to verified instructions: ________
  • First payment and servicing contact verified: ________
  • Recording, refunds, corrections and retention owner: ________

Frequently asked questions

When should I receive the Closing Disclosure?

CFPB states that for most covered mortgages the lender must provide it at least three business days before the scheduled closing. Use the actual transaction documents and lender to confirm timing and any revised disclosure.

Should every fee match the Loan Estimate exactly?

Not necessarily. Different fee categories have different change rules, and permitted circumstances can matter. Record each difference and ask the lender which rule and fact support it.

Is cash to close the same as closing costs?

No. Cash to close incorporates the down payment and other adjustments, deposits and credits in addition to closing costs. Reconstruct it from the form rather than treating one total as the other.

What if I find an error on closing day?

Pause and get the lender or settlement agent to address it. CFPB advises getting errors fixed before closing and not signing documents you do not understand; qualified legal advice may be appropriate.
