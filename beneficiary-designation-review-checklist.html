Beneficiary Designation Review: Accounts, Forms, Proof and Follow-Through

A name in a will, an old spreadsheet or a conversation is not the same as an accepted account designation. Build a dated register, use each institution's current process, verify what was recorded, and route conflicts or complex family facts to qualified counsel.

Published November 5, 2026. General U.S. recordkeeping education, not legal, tax, estate-planning, investment or insurance advice. Account terms, marital rights and state law vary; use current institution documents and qualified professionals for consequential decisions.

The useful answer

  • Inventory every account that can carry a beneficiary instead of assuming one estate document controls them all.
  • Record primary and contingent choices, allocation method, legal names, relationship and the institution's current requirements without storing unnecessary sensitive identifiers.
  • Submit changes only through the account provider or plan administrator, then obtain dated confirmation that the designation was accepted—not merely started.
  • Review after marriage, divorce, birth, adoption, death, account transfer, employer change or a material estate-plan update.
  • Pause for professional advice when a minor, trust, estate, charity, disability benefit, former spouse, court order, tax issue or family conflict is involved.

Build the account register before changing a name

Start with accounts, not people. List each employer retirement plan, IRA, annuity, life-insurance policy, bank account with a payable-on-death feature, brokerage account with transfer-on-death registration, equity award, pension or other contract that may carry a beneficiary. Record institution, plan or policy name, partial account identifier, owner, account type, portal or service route and the date checked. Do not copy full account numbers, Social Security numbers or passwords into a casual worksheet.

Mark the current record as confirmed, missing, unclear or not offered. A beneficiary shown on one account says nothing about another account at the same company. A transferred IRA, replaced policy, new employer plan or merged bank can create a new record even when the old choice felt settled. Investor.gov notes that brokerage firms may choose whether to offer transfer-on-death registration and that state law governs registration, so verify the exact account rather than assuming a universal option.

Keep this register with the household-record system, but store the sensitive confirmation documents in the secure location used for financial and estate records. The register should point to evidence, not become a high-value identity file itself.

  • Institution, account type and partial identifier: ________
  • Beneficiary feature offered and record located: yes / no / unclear
  • Current primary and contingent status: ________
  • Secure confirmation location: ________

Separate primary, contingent and allocation decisions

A primary beneficiary is first in line under the account's terms. A contingent beneficiary generally receives the benefit when no primary beneficiary qualifies under those terms. When several people or entities are named, the form may require percentages, equal shares or a distribution method. Do not improvise legal phrases such as per stirpes, per capita, representation or descendants without understanding the provider's definition and the governing law.

Use complete legal names and the exact relationship or entity information requested. Confirm whether the institution requires dates of birth, tax identifiers, addresses or trust details, and provide them through its protected process rather than email or a shared note. If a named person has died, a charity has changed legal identity or a trust has been amended, stop and obtain the current instructions before submitting a replacement.

Minors, people receiving means-tested benefits, trusts, estates and charities raise administration, control and tax questions that a simple form cannot solve. The NAIC advises extra care with minors and trusts on life-insurance policies. Treat those situations as a legal-review trigger, not a reason to guess at a custodian or beneficiary label.

  • Primary beneficiary or beneficiaries and allocation: ________
  • Contingent beneficiary or beneficiaries and allocation: ________
  • Provider definition for distribution language reviewed: yes / no
  • Minor, trust, estate, charity or benefit issue routed for advice: ________

Respect spouse rights, plan documents and court orders

Retirement plans can have special surviving-spouse protections. The IRS directs married participants to review plan beneficiaries and notes that many plans require a spouse's written consent to name another beneficiary or choose a different payment form. The Department of Labor explains that most 401(k) plans and certain other plans generally protect a surviving spouse unless a valid waiver is completed and properly witnessed. The exact rule depends on the plan and facts.

Divorce does not make every old designation disappear, and a divorce decree or qualified domestic relations order may preserve rights for a former spouse. Remarriage can create new rights. Do not rely on a generic statement that a will, divorce or new marriage automatically overrides every account form. Provide the plan administrator and qualified attorney with the relevant plan document, decree, order and current designation before acting.

Ask for the summary plan description and the provider's beneficiary procedures. Record whether spouse consent is required, how it must be witnessed, whether electronic submission is permitted and what happens if no valid beneficiary survives. Do not ask a customer-service representative to give legal advice; ask them to identify the controlling plan document and process.

  • Marital status and recent change reviewed: ________
  • Plan spouse-consent rule and witness method: ________
  • Divorce decree, QDRO or other order reviewed by qualified counsel: ________
  • Controlling document and administrator contact saved: ________

Submit through the provider and prove acceptance

Use the institution's authenticated portal, signed form or other published route. Confirm the exact account, effective date, beneficiary class, allocation total and required signatures before submission. Save a clean copy or confirmation screen, the submission date and the channel used. A draft form, open browser tab or outbound envelope is not proof that the institution accepted the change.

After the stated processing time, retrieve the account's beneficiary summary or request written confirmation. Compare it character by character with the intended record and resolve truncation, missing contingents, rejected percentages or an unprocessed spouse consent. Record the representative, case number and promised correction date when follow-up is needed. Avoid sending beneficiary data through an unverified link in a text or unexpected email; sign in through the known institution route.

For a bank payable-on-death account, confirm that beneficiaries are actually named in the bank's deposit records. FDIC materials explain that naming in institution records matters for treatment as an informal revocable trust account; the insurance calculation is separate from estate-planning suitability. For brokerage transfer-on-death registration, confirm the firm offers it and that the account registration, not merely a contact profile, changed.

  • Submitted through authenticated route on: ________
  • Allocation validated and signatures complete: yes / no
  • Accepted designation confirmed on: ________
  • Correction case, owner and deadline: ________

Reconcile the register with the larger plan

Compare accepted designations with the current will, trust, insurance plan, retirement plan and household liquidity needs. The goal is not to make every document use the same names; it is to identify intentional differences and accidental conflicts. Account contracts can transfer assets outside probate, while other property may move under a will, trust, title or state law. A qualified estate attorney can explain which document governs each asset in your jurisdiction.

Check whether the proposed recipient can practically receive and manage the asset, whether a trust is correctly identified, whether a charity's legal name and tax identifier are current, and whether the choice creates an unintended tax, creditor, benefits or family consequence. Do not turn a beneficiary review into an investment sale or rollover unless that transaction is independently appropriate and understood.

Tell an executor, trustee or trusted contact where provider names and records can be found without giving them passwords or unnecessary account access. The NAIC recommends that life-insurance beneficiaries or trusted advisers know the insurer's name and that a current policy copy be kept with estate papers. Preserve access instructions, not live credentials.

  • Will, trust, title and account records reconciled: ________
  • Intentional differences documented: ________
  • Professional questions and owner: ________
  • Trusted person knows record location: yes / no

Set review triggers and close the loop

Review the register at a fixed annual checkpoint and after marriage, divorce, separation, birth, adoption, death, disability, a child's adulthood, a move across state lines, employer change, account transfer, policy replacement, trust amendment or material tax-law change. A review does not require a change. It requires confirming that the institution still shows the intended record and that the surrounding facts have not made it obsolete.

For each account, record checked date, evidence location, unresolved issue, next action, owner and deadline. Close only when the provider's record matches the decision or a qualified adviser has documented a different next step. Retain superseded confirmations according to the household-record policy so a later dispute can show what changed and when, then securely destroy redundant copies when no legal hold or continuing purpose remains.

This checklist is a control file, not an estate plan. Its value is making gaps visible early: an empty contingent field, a deceased recipient, an old employer plan, an unaccepted form or a conflict that needs advice while the owner can still explain intent.

  • Annual review month: ________
  • Life-event trigger and date: ________
  • Open issue, owner and deadline: ________
  • Register closed with accepted proof: yes / no

Frequently asked questions

Does a will override every beneficiary designation?

Do not assume so. Retirement plans, insurance policies and transfer-on-death or payable-on-death accounts can be governed by their own contracts, federal rules and state law. Ask qualified counsel which document controls each asset.

Should I name minor children directly?

Get qualified estate and insurance advice first. Providers may not pay proceeds directly to a minor, and an improvised designation can create court, custodial or benefits complications.

Is saving a submitted form enough?

No. Keep the submitted copy, then obtain dated confirmation that the institution accepted and recorded the designation on the correct account.

When should beneficiaries be reviewed?

At least on a regular annual checkpoint and after marriage, divorce, birth, adoption, death, account transfer, employer change, policy replacement, trust amendment or another material life or legal change.
