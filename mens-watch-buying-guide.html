Men’s Watch Buying Guide: Fit, Movement, Water Resistance and Service

A watch is a small machine worn at a difficult angle. Start with the wrist and the job, then verify the exact reference instead of buying a diameter and a story.

Published September 22, 2026. Independent purchase guidance. Accuracy, water resistance, power reserve, service intervals and warranty terms are model-specific; verify the current maker documentation for the exact reference.

The useful answer

  • Measure wrist circumference and the flat top surface, then compare case width, lug-to-lug length, thickness and total fitted length as a system.
  • Choose quartz, solar-powered quartz or mechanical by the maintenance and wearing experience you actually want—not a hierarchy of prestige.
  • Treat water-resistance markings as model-specific operating limits, not a depth invitation; read the manual and have seals checked as the maker directs.
  • Verify strap width, clasp range, crown operation, crystal, dial legibility, warranty route and service support before checkout.
  • Save the reference number, seller record, warranty and instructions; condition and provenance matter more than a vague collectible claim.

Define the job before the watch

Write the ordinary week: office, formal events, travel, workshop, swimming, running or an all-purpose daily watch. Note the clothing cuffs it must clear, whether timing at a glance matters, and whether you will tolerate resetting it after days off. A dress watch, field-style watch, dive-style watch and connected sports watch can all tell time while solving very different comfort, durability and information problems.

Set the maintenance appetite alongside the budget. Include straps, batteries or rechargeable-cell service, pressure testing, regulation, overhaul, shipping and the possibility that a low-cost movement is replaced rather than repaired. A purchase price without an ownership plan makes a complicated watch look artificially cheap and a simple one artificially plain.

Decide which claims matter enough to verify. If the watch will enter water, locate the exact manual and water-resistance table. If accuracy matters, find the stated tolerance for the exact movement. If heritage or origin drives the price, look for a maker source rather than marketplace copy. Do not pay for a story you cannot connect to the reference number.

Measure the wrist in four dimensions

Measure wrist circumference at the wearing point with a flexible tape, snug but not tight. Then look at the flat top surface between the wrist edges. Case diameter is only one dimension: lug-to-lug length determines how far the watch spans, thickness controls cuff clearance, and case or lug shape affects whether the ends follow the wrist or project beyond it.

A short-lugged larger case can fit better than a smaller case with long straight lugs. Integrated bracelets and fixed end links can create a longer effective span than the case specification suggests. Ask for the true lug-to-lug measurement and, where relevant, the first rigid-link span. Product photos on a model wrist are not a substitute for numbers or a try-on.

Check total wearable range, not just strap length. A bracelet needs enough removable links and useful micro-adjustment; a leather or fabric strap needs the correct hole position without an excessive tail. Leave normal allowance for heat and daily swelling. The watch should not cut circulation, slide onto the hand or rotate so freely that the crown and lugs strike the wrist.

Choose a movement without prestige fog

Conventional quartz is usually the straightforward choice when low attention and easy timekeeping matter. Solar-powered quartz converts light to stored energy but still has model-specific charging, storage and eventual service requirements. Mechanical watches trade some convenience and usually some accuracy for a movement that is wound by the crown, wrist motion or both. None of these types is automatically more honest, durable or appropriate.

Read the exact caliber instructions. Confirm battery or charging behavior, expected accuracy language, power reserve, calendar correction limits, hand-setting procedure and whether the seconds hand has a low-energy warning. A retailer may group several references under one listing even when movements differ. The case-back or maker reference should lead to the correct manual.

Mechanical accuracy changes with position, temperature, power state, wear and magnetism. Seiko's customer guidance, for example, distinguishes how magnetism can affect quartz and mechanical movements and recommends professional attention when a watch is affected. Treat a published tolerance as a range under stated conditions, not a promise that every daily reading will be identical.

Read water resistance as an operating instruction

Water-resistance language belongs to the exact watch, manual and current condition. A pressure or depth marking describes a test framework; it is not a recommendation to take the watch to that physical depth or expose it to every water activity. Dynamic movement, temperature changes, soap, crown position, impact and aging gaskets can change the real situation.

Find the maker's table for the precise marking and follow it. Confirm whether swimming, showering or diving is allowed; whether buttons or a crown may be operated when wet; and whether a screw-down crown must be secured. Hot tubs, saunas and detergents add heat and chemistry that a simple pressure number does not explain. When the manual is missing, assume less rather than inventing permission.

Water resistance is not necessarily permanent. Gaskets age and a case opened for service must be closed and tested correctly. Follow the maker's inspection and pressure-testing schedule, especially before planned water use or after impact or service. Fogging or moisture under the crystal calls for prompt qualified service, not a bowl of rice or another swim.

Inspect the interface: dial, crystal, crown and strap

Test legibility at the angles and light levels that matter. Hands need contrast against the dial, minute markers need enough precision for the use, and luminous material needs scope rather than a dramatic studio photograph. Complications add information but also add setting steps and possible service cost. A date window you cannot read or keep correct is not free utility.

Crystal materials trade scratch behavior, impact behavior, reflections and price. Ask what is actually fitted and whether an anti-reflective coating is external, internal or both. Inspect the crown for grip and clearance; a large crown can be convenient to set yet press into the hand. Pushers should match the intended function and should never be operated in water unless the manual expressly permits it.

Record lug width and the attachment system before planning strap changes. Quick-release bars are convenient only when compatible and secure; drilled lugs help some changes but still require care. Curved ends, proprietary bars and integrated bracelets can sharply limit replacements. Check buckle finish, clasp security, keeper placement and whether replacement components exist for the exact model.

Verify seller, reference and warranty before paying

Match the full reference number on the listing, invoice, tag and maker documentation. Confirm who the seller is, whether the maker recognizes that sales channel, what accessories and papers are included, and how a return is evaluated after sizing or wear. Marketplace storefront names can obscure the actual merchant, and a stock image may represent a sibling reference with a different size or movement.

Read the written warranty before purchase. FTC consumer guidance says written warranty terms must be available before you buy and recommends checking duration, coverage, exclusions, claim process, labor or shipping charges, and the company's promised remedy. Save the warranty and receipt. Get any important spoken promise in writing rather than treating a salesperson's sentence as a service network.

Separate the manufacturer's warranty from a paid service contract and from a seller's return policy. Ask who handles routine service, water testing and parts after the warranty. A watch with an impressive specification but no realistic support route may be the wrong daily tool. Search the exact brand and reference in recall and safety-warning sources before checkout.

Use a disciplined try-on and arrival check

At a store, ask before operating or winding the watch. Try it with the clothing and wrist position you use, then view it from the side as well as above. Check whether lugs overhang, the crown presses, the clasp lands on the underside of the wrist, and the dial remains readable without rotating the arm unnaturally. Photograph the reference label, not another customer's serial number.

For an online order, keep films, links and packaging intact until the return terms are understood. In a clean dry area, match the reference, inspect the case and crystal, test the clasp, confirm included documentation and follow the manual for setup. Do not perform a home immersion test; it can create damage without establishing certified resistance.

Timekeeping checks need an agreed interval, correct initial synchronization and the model's stated tolerance. A brief observation cannot prove long-term performance. If the watch arrives stopped, damaged, mismatched or materially different from the listing, preserve photos and contact the seller before wearing, sizing or opening the case.

Maintain the watch you actually bought

Follow the model's cleaning instructions. Wipe sweat and salt from compatible exterior surfaces, keep leather and other moisture-sensitive straps dry, and do not use solvents or ultrasonic equipment without maker approval. Rinse only when the exact instructions and current water resistance permit it. A watch is not made safer by generic internet cleaning advice.

Keep it away from strong magnetic sources and hard impacts, and store it where temperature, moisture and charging conditions match the manual. Do not leave a solar watch permanently in darkness or overheat it in direct sun. Do not open the case to replace a battery or gasket unless you have the tools, skill and test equipment required for that reference.

Create a simple record: reference and serial, purchase date, seller, warranty, manuals, strap sizes, service dates, pressure-test results and observed timing. That record supports maintenance, resale disclosure and troubleshooting without pretending the watch is an investment. Buy the piece whose fit, function and service path remain convincing after the romance is removed.

Frequently asked questions

What watch size fits my wrist?

There is no single diameter rule. Compare wrist shape and circumference with case width, lug-to-lug length, thickness, strap or bracelet range and the watch's effective rigid span.

Is quartz or mechanical better?

Quartz generally favors low attention and accuracy; mechanical favors a particular wearing and maintenance experience. Solar quartz changes the energy system but still has model-specific care. Choose the tradeoff you want.

Can I swim with a water-resistant watch?

Only when the exact maker instructions, marking and current condition permit that activity. Secure the crown as directed and have seals or pressure resistance checked on the maker's schedule.

Should I buy a watch as an investment?

Treat it first as a wearable product with costs, condition risk and uncertain resale. Do not rely on generalized appreciation claims or a seller's prediction when deciding what you can afford.
