Missing Package and Delivery Claim File: Track, Report, Dispute and Close

A delivery scan is a clue, not a complete case. Freeze the order record, separate delay from loss or theft, give the seller and carrier the facts they each control, protect payment deadlines and close every duplicate promise in writing.

Published November 1, 2026. General U.S. consumer-organization guidance, not legal, postal, insurance or payment advice. Seller terms, carrier rules, card protections, deadlines and local theft-reporting procedures vary. Use current official instructions and qualified help for a valuable, sensitive, dangerous or suspected criminal shipment.

The useful answer

  • Save the listing, order confirmation, promised shipment date, payment record, tracking history, address and every delivery communication before pages or scans change.
  • Classify the problem accurately: not shipped, delayed, delivered to the wrong place, marked delivered but absent, damaged, opened, incomplete or an order you never placed.
  • Contact the seller first for the purchase remedy while using the carrier’s official search or claim path for movement and delivery evidence.
  • Do not let a slow support thread consume a credit-card, debit-card, platform, carrier-insurance or police-report deadline.
  • Close the case only after the refund, replacement, claim, return, account security and later-arriving original package are reconciled.

Freeze the transaction before opening a support chat

Create one case ID and save the seller, exact item, variation, quantity, price, tax, shipping charge, payment method, order number, order date, promised ship and delivery dates, delivery address and cancellation or refund terms. Save the product page and confirmation as a PDF or screenshot, but keep the original email and statement. Record the name shown on the charge because a marketplace, merchant and payment descriptor may differ.

Copy the full tracking history with date, time, facility, exception, out-for-delivery and delivered events. A scan can be corrected or supplemented later, so preserve the current state and the public tracking URL without publishing private addresses or access codes. If the order includes several boxes, assign a tracking number and expected contents to each one. Do not call an incomplete multi-box shipment a total loss.

  • Order lane: seller, marketplace, item, amount, promised date and refund terms.
  • Parcel lane: carrier, tracking number, package count, weight if shown and scan history.
  • Payment lane: card or account, statement date, charge descriptor and dispute contact route.
  • Evidence lane: messages, photographs, doorbell clips, building logs and case numbers.

Name the failure before choosing the remedy

Not shipped means the seller has not handed a package into a carrier network. Delayed means movement or an exception still appears. Misdelivered means credible evidence points to another address or handoff. Marked delivered but absent means the scan exists but possession is unresolved. Damaged, opened or missing contents is an evidence-preservation problem. An order you never placed is an account and payment-security incident. Keep these lanes separate because a reshipment, carrier search, payment dispute and fraud response solve different failures.

Check the order address, unit, postal code and saved account address before alleging theft. Look at the delivery location promised by the seller, carrier photograph if one is supplied, safe-place note, parcel locker, building desk, household handoff and nearby entrances only where safe and lawful. Do not confront a neighbor, enter private property, publish surveillance footage or represent a delivery photo as conclusive when the address or parcel identity is not visible.

Give the seller one complete purchase record

The seller owns the agreement to provide the merchandise or an appropriate remedy. Send a concise written notice with the order number, exact missing item, promised date, tracking number, current status, delivery address confirmation, checks already completed and the outcome requested. Ask for the next action, owner and date. Keep chat transcripts and automated emails; a case marked resolved in a portal is not the same as a refund posted or replacement received.

FTC guidance says covered mail, internet and telephone sellers must ship when promised or, when no time is stated, generally within thirty days; delays require the buyer’s consent or a cancellation and prompt refund. That rule concerns shipment promises, not proof that every delivered scan is accurate. Apply the current rule and exclusions to the exact transaction, and contact the FTC or qualified counsel rather than inventing a right for a marketplace, custom item or service that may sit outside the rule.

  • Requested outcome: trace, replacement, refund, missing-part shipment or written denial.
  • Owner: seller, marketplace, carrier or payment issuer—not an unnamed support team.
  • Date: next update and the last day for each independent claim or dispute route.
  • Closure proof: posted credit, replacement tracking, signed claim decision or completed return.

Use the carrier path without surrendering the seller path

Follow the exact carrier’s current missing-package sequence. USPS, for example, directs customers to check tracking, submit an online help request and, when the stated waiting period is met, submit a Missing Mail search with sender and recipient addresses, container details, tracking or mailing evidence, a contents description and useful photographs. Carrier insurance claims may belong to the sender, recipient or account holder depending on the service. Do not assume the person who paid for the item is automatically the carrier claimant.

Ask for factual delivery evidence: scan time, service level, delivery point, locker or signature record, parcel description, weight or package count where available, and the carrier case number. Do not ask an agent to declare theft without evidence. If the parcel contained medication, identity documents, hazardous goods, high-value property or time-sensitive materials, use the seller, carrier, prescriber, issuing agency, insurer or law-enforcement route appropriate to that item instead of treating it as an ordinary retail delay.

Protect payment and fraud deadlines in parallel

If the seller does not correct a non-delivery and the charge appears, review the issuer’s current dispute instructions immediately. FTC consumer guidance distinguishes credit- and debit-card protections and notes that certain credit-card billing errors must be disputed in writing within sixty days after the first statement containing the error. An issuer may offer an online path, but do not assume a support chat preserves every statutory protection. Send copies, not originals, to the address or route specified for billing disputes.

A package ordered through a compromised account needs more than a delivery claim. Change the affected account password from a trusted device, review saved addresses, payment methods, forwarding rules, active sessions and related email security, and contact the payment issuer. Preserve the fraudulent order and messages before deleting anything. Report suspected fraud through the FTC route and local law enforcement when appropriate; use the carrier case as evidence, not as a substitute for account recovery.

Close the case and prevent a double remedy

Maintain a line for each remedy: seller refund, marketplace credit, replacement shipment, carrier claim, payment dispute, insurance claim and police report. Record amount, date, status and owner. If the original package arrives after a refund or replacement, stop using or opening it until the seller gives written return, keep or payment instructions. A duplicate remedy can create a new dispute and destroy an otherwise clean record.

Close only when money has posted, a replacement has been accepted, a denied claim has been reviewed or the chosen escalation path is complete. Save the final statement, receipt, tracking outcome and correspondence with the purchase record. Then update the delivery plan: correct stale addresses, remove obsolete access notes, use a staffed counter or locker for sensitive items where available, and avoid putting unnecessary personal details in delivery instructions.

Frequently asked questions

Does a delivered scan prove that I received the package?

It is evidence of a carrier event, not necessarily proof of the exact address, recipient, parcel condition or contents. Preserve it and request the carrier’s available delivery details.

Should I contact the seller or carrier first?

Contact the seller for the purchase remedy and use the carrier’s official search path for movement and delivery evidence. Often both paths should run together with one shared record.

Can I dispute a credit-card charge for merchandise that never arrived?

FTC guidance identifies certain undelivered-as-agreed charges as potential billing errors, but timing and written-notice rules matter. Follow the issuer’s current official instructions promptly.

What if the missing package arrives after a refund?

Keep it unopened when practical and ask the seller in writing whether to return, keep or repay for it. Preserve the response so the case does not produce a duplicate remedy.
