Frozen Pipe Prevention and Thawing Guide: Map, Shut Off and Respond

A frozen line is not merely an inconvenience; it can split where you cannot see it and release water when the ice thaws. Prepare the shutoff and the map before the cold, then use a conservative decision record when flow disappears.

Published October 18, 2026. General household preparedness, not plumbing, electrical, fire, building-code or insurance advice. Systems and local rules vary. Leave, call emergency services and follow utility or responder instructions for electrical hazards, fire, structural danger, major flooding or any condition you cannot control safely; use a licensed plumber for inaccessible, damaged or uncertain piping.

The useful answer

  • Map pipes in exterior walls, crawl spaces, attics, garages and cabinets; label the main water shutoff and confirm every adult can reach and operate it safely.
  • Before a freeze, disconnect exterior hoses, follow local winterization guidance, close garage doors around water lines, protect exposed pipe and keep safe heat reaching vulnerable areas.
  • Loss of flow is a warning, not proof that a pipe is intact. Look and listen for leakage, know when to shut off the main, and call a plumber when the frozen section is hidden or unsafe.
  • When an accessible pipe is appropriate to thaw, keep the faucet open and use controlled heat such as an electric heating pad, hair dryer or warm towels; never use a torch or other open flame.
  • After flow returns, inspect slowly and continuously for leaks, document damage and repairs, and do not restore water or electricity into an unsafe wet area.

Create the pipe and shutoff map before the weather turns

Trace supply lines where practical from the main entry to fixtures, water-using appliances and exterior faucets. Mark sections near exterior walls, unheated crawl spaces, attics, garages, cabinets, additions and openings where cold air can reach plumbing. Note pipes that froze before and rooms that lose heat during an outage. Do not remove building material or enter a confined, wet or structurally unsafe space to complete the map; uncertainty is itself a reason to involve a qualified plumber.

Locate and label the main water shutoff and any safe fixture or branch valves. Clear access, add adequate lighting and record the direction and tool needed, if any. Do not force a seized valve during an emergency drill. Ask the water utility or a plumber which valve the occupant may operate and how a fire-sprinkler or shared-building system changes the plan. Photograph the route and put the utility, plumber, landlord or property manager, insurer and electrician contacts with the home emergency file.

Reduce exposure before a freeze

Disconnect, drain and store garden hoses and follow the manufacturer, utility or plumber instructions for exterior faucets, irrigation and seasonal systems. Insulate accessible vulnerable pipe with material suitable for that pipe and location, but do not treat insulation as a substitute for heat or repair. Seal cold-air leaks around penetrations only with methods appropriate to the assembly; avoid blocking required combustion air, vents, drains or access. Keep garage doors closed when water lines run through the garage.

Maintain indoor heat and safe air circulation. Red Cross guidance recommends opening kitchen and bathroom cabinet doors so warmer air can circulate around plumbing, while moving harmful cleaners out of children’s reach. If the weather and the known pipe route justify it, a small trickle from the faucet served by an exposed line may help prevent freezing; follow the water utility’s current local advice and understand the water cost. Never use an oven, grill or outdoor combustion appliance to heat the home, and keep space-heater use within current fire-safety guidance.

Plan for vacancy and power loss

A vacant house needs an explicit temperature, water and monitoring plan. IBHS recommends keeping heat at least 55°F when away and knowing the main shutoff; policies, leases, systems and climates can impose different duties. If an extended absence overlaps subfreezing weather, ask the insurer, utility and plumber whether shutting off and properly draining domestic lines is appropriate. Do not shut down a fire-protection, boiler, well or shared system without qualified direction.

During a power outage, indoor temperature can fall before pipes show symptoms. Use safe outage heat only as designed, monitor the coldest pipe areas and keep the shutoff route lit and clear. A low-temperature or leak alarm can add notice, but only if its sensor location, connectivity, battery and response contact are tested. Arrange a person who can physically respond; an alert that reaches a phone hundreds of miles away is not a shutoff. Record who has permission and keys before the trip begins.

When flow stops, run the safety gate first

If a faucet produces only a trickle during freezing weather, suspect a frozen line. Check other fixtures to understand the scope, then look and listen for water, bulging, cracks, staining, ceiling changes or unusual pump activity. A pipe can split while frozen and reveal the leak only during thaw. If water is escaping or damage is suspected, shut off the appropriate safe supply if you know how, move away from electrical hazards and call the plumber or emergency service appropriate to the condition.

Do not start thawing a pipe hidden in a wall, ceiling, floor, crawl space or inaccessible area. Call a licensed plumber if the frozen section cannot be located, the pipe is damaged, the system is unfamiliar, a safe shutoff is unavailable or standing water, electricity, fire-sprinkler piping, a boiler or structural damage is involved. Notify a landlord or property manager promptly when the system is not yours to alter. Time spent proving self-reliance is not worth a concealed leak or fire.

Thaw only an accessible, intact pipe with controlled heat

When the pipe is accessible, appears intact and the situation is otherwise safe, Red Cross guidance says to keep the affected faucet open and apply heat to the frozen section using an electric heating pad, an electric hair dryer, a portable space heater kept away from flammable materials, or towels soaked in hot water. Begin near the faucet and continue toward the colder section so melting water and steam have an open route. Follow every appliance instruction and keep cords and devices away from water.

Never use a blowtorch, kerosene or propane heater, charcoal stove or another open-flame device on or near the pipe. Excess heat can damage piping and ignite nearby materials. Do not leave a heat source unattended or put an ordinary electrical device into a wet area. If flow does not return, the pipe becomes too hot to touch safely, a leak appears or any part of the situation changes, stop and call the plumber. A slow professional repair is better than converting a freeze into a fire.

Restore slowly, document the incident and remove the cause

After flow returns, keep watching the entire accessible route for leaks while water pressure normalizes. If the main was closed, have the appropriate qualified person advise how and when to restore it; open valves slowly and never energize wet electrical equipment or enter standing water with a possible electrical source. Catch small water only when it is safe, move undamaged property away, and use the site’s flood-cleanup guide after the immediate plumbing and electrical hazards are controlled.

Photograph the location, damage and temporary controls before disposal or repair when safe, save weather and alert records, and contact the insurer promptly for claim instructions rather than assuming coverage. Keep invoices, moisture and drying records, replaced parts and the plumber’s cause assessment. Then update the map: where did cold enter, why did heat fail, and what permanent insulation, air-sealing, rerouting, monitoring or service is recommended? Close the event only when the cause and the next freeze plan have owners and dates.

Frequently asked questions

Should I leave a faucet dripping in freezing weather?

A small trickle through a pipe exposed to severe cold can help, according to Red Cross guidance, but it is not a universal substitute for heat, insulation or repairs. Use the faucet served by the vulnerable line and follow current local utility guidance.

Can I use a torch to thaw a frozen pipe?

No. Red Cross guidance says never use a blowtorch, kerosene or propane heater, charcoal stove or other open-flame device. Use controlled methods only when the pipe is accessible and intact, or call a licensed plumber.

Does a frozen pipe always burst?

No, but loss of flow does not prove the pipe is intact. A split can remain hidden until thaw restores water pressure, so inspect continuously and know how to shut off the supply.

When should I call a plumber for a frozen pipe?

Call when the frozen section is hidden or cannot be located, the pipe may be damaged, flow does not return, the system is unfamiliar, or water, electricity, structural damage, boilers, wells, sprinklers or shared plumbing make the situation unsafe or unclear.
