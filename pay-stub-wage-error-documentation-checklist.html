Pay Stub and Wage Error Documentation Checklist: Hours, Rates, Deductions and Escalation

A confusing paycheck becomes manageable when every number has a source, every question belongs to the right agency, and every correction is preserved in writing. Reconcile one pay period first; widen the review only when the evidence supports it.

Published November 4, 2026. General U.S. recordkeeping and consumer education, not legal, tax, benefits or employment advice. Pay-stub duties, wage rules, deadlines and remedies vary by worker status, job, contract and jurisdiction. Protect sensitive payroll data and obtain qualified help when rights, retaliation, taxes or benefits are at issue.

The useful answer

  • Freeze the original stub, time record, schedule, rate notice, deposit record and relevant messages before requesting a correction.
  • Reconcile pay period, hours, pay basis, regular and overtime earnings, additions, deductions, taxes, net pay and year-to-date figures as separate lanes.
  • Write the smallest provable discrepancy first, cite the source record for each number and ask payroll for a written calculation or corrected statement.
  • Route wage, state-law, tax, benefits and banking questions separately; one agency or department does not control every pay-stub line.
  • Close only when the corrected amount, deposit, stub, tax and benefit records agree and the correction date and retention file are recorded.

Freeze the source packet before doing arithmetic

Save the pay stub exactly as delivered, including every page, legend, pay code and year-to-date column. Add the schedule, clock or timesheet record, approved leave, rate or salary notice, offer letter or applicable agreement, tip or commission record, expense reimbursement, bonus notice, benefit election, garnishment notice when relevant, and the bank record showing the actual deposit. Annotate copies and keep originals unchanged so a later system correction does not erase the starting point.

Label the employer’s legal name, work location, payroll provider, employee identifier, pay date and pay-period start and end. Protect Social Security numbers, bank details and health information; do not email an unredacted packet to an unverified address. The Department of Labor says covered employers must keep accurate wage and hour records, but federal law does not settle every promised-wage, pay-stub, deduction, break or final-pay question. Record the state and worker classification before choosing a remedy.

  • Original stub, time record and deposit saved: ________
  • Pay period, pay date, employer and work location: ________
  • Rate, leave, commission or bonus source: ________
  • Sensitive fields redacted from working copies: yes / no

Rebuild the time lane without changing the record

Create one row for each workday: scheduled start and end, actual start and end, meal or other unpaid interval, approved paid leave, location, job or project code, and the source for each entry. Do not overwrite an employer system or recreate memory as fact. If a punch is missing, mark it missing and attach the contemporaneous calendar, message, work product or witness information that helps explain it.

Total hours by the employer’s stated workweek, not merely by the pay period. Federal overtime rules for covered nonexempt work generally operate by workweek, while exemptions, state rules, contracts and special industries can change the analysis. Record rather than assume whether training, travel, on-call time, pre- or post-shift work, meal periods or remote work belongs in paid time; those are classification questions for policy, law or qualified review.

  • Employer-defined workweek begins: ________
  • Daily actual hours and source record: ________
  • Missing, edited or disputed entries: ________
  • Unresolved time-category question: ________

Reconcile earnings from basis to net pay

Write the pay basis exactly as stated: hourly rate, salary, piece rate, commission formula or another method. Separate straight-time earnings, overtime, shift differentials, tips, commissions, bonuses, paid leave, reimbursements and every other addition. For each line, record quantity, rate, expected amount, stub amount and difference. Do not force a gross-to-net result by inserting an unexplained adjustment merely because the final deposit happens to match.

List every deduction and tax line with the displayed label, current amount and year-to-date amount. Distinguish taxes, employee benefit contributions, retirement deferrals, garnishments, advances, dues and other deductions. A familiar label is not proof the amount is right, and a dispute may belong to payroll, a plan administrator, a court order, a tax authority or a labor agency. Check net pay last against all actual payments, including split deposits or a paper check.

  • Expected gross from documented components: ________
  • Stub gross and unexplained difference: ________
  • Deductions with source authorization or notice: ________
  • Net pay, split deposits and actual receipt reconciled: ________

Send a narrow correction request with a deadline

Contact the official payroll or human-resources channel and state the pay period, affected line, expected figure, displayed figure, difference and supporting record. Ask for the calculation, pay-code definition, correction amount, payment date, corrected stub, year-to-date treatment and any downstream tax or benefit correction. Keep the tone factual. A screenshot with a circled number is less useful than a short table another person can reproduce.

Save the submission, confirmation number, attachments and every response. If the employer asks you to alter a time record, preserve the prior version and ask what changed, who approved it and why. Do not sign a statement you know is inaccurate. If payment is urgent, retaliation is feared, access is cut off, the employer closes, or a deadline may run, contact the appropriate agency or qualified adviser promptly instead of waiting indefinitely for an informal reply.

  • Disputed line, expected amount and evidence: ________
  • Official recipient and case number: ________
  • Requested written calculation and correction date: ________
  • Next review or escalation date: ________

Choose the correct escalation lane

For possible federal minimum-wage, overtime or FLSA recordkeeping issues, the Department of Labor Wage and Hour Division provides confidential information and complaint routes. State labor agencies may cover pay stubs, promised wages, deductions, breaks, final pay and state minimum-wage or overtime rules that federal law does not. A union, public-sector employer, government contract or industry-specific rule may create another path. Record which rule and agency you are relying on rather than sending the same packet everywhere.

Tax forms use a different lane. The IRS directs employees to ask the employer to correct an inaccurate Form W-2 and explains the later IRS and Form 4852 process if it remains unresolved. Benefit contribution or coverage errors may require the plan administrator and plan documents; a missing or wrong bank deposit may also require payroll and the bank. Preserve the distinction between a pay calculation error, unauthorized transaction, incorrect tax form and benefits-posting problem.

  • Federal wage-and-hour question: ________
  • State labor or contract question: ________
  • Tax-form, benefits or banking question: ________
  • Deadline and qualified contact verified: ________

Close the correction across every affected record

A make-up deposit is not the end if the pay stub, year-to-date wages, taxes, retirement contributions, leave balance or benefit deductions remain wrong. Match the correction notice to the new payment, corrected statement and subsequent stub. Record whether the correction was regular wages, reimbursement, reversal or another category, and ask how it will appear on the Form W-2 or other year-end record.

Keep a closure sheet showing issue, amount, pay periods, documents, contacts, payments, corrected fields and remaining questions. Retain records for the period required by applicable law, tax guidance, plan rules, contract or professional advice. Remove shared links, store originals securely and keep a redacted working copy. The file is closed only when a third party can follow the chronology and the affected systems agree.

  • Corrected stub and year-to-date totals verified: ________
  • Tax, leave, retirement and benefit effects reconciled: ________
  • Secure retention date and archive location: ________

Frequently asked questions

What should I save when a paycheck looks wrong?

Save the original stub, time and schedule records, rate or policy document, approvals and messages, benefit or deduction records, and the actual deposit before requesting changes.

Does federal law require every worker to receive a pay stub?

The FLSA requires covered employers to keep specified wage and hour records, but it does not create a universal federal pay-stub rule. State law, worker status and other rules may apply.

Where do I report a wage error?

Start with the employer’s official correction route when appropriate. Federal wage-and-hour issues may go to DOL Wage and Hour; state wage, deduction or pay-stub issues may belong to the state labor agency. Tax and benefit errors use different routes.

What if an incorrect paycheck also caused fees?

Document the deposit timing, account transaction and fee separately. Ask the employer and bank for their written handling, but do not assume a wage correction automatically resolves a banking-fee claim or legal remedy.
