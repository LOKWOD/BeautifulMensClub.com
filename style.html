THE FIRST PRINCIPLE

Fit is the difference between “nice shirt” and “you look good.”

Price is invisible. Fit is not. Before buying anything else, learn the five places clothes tell on you.

Shoulders

The seam should end where your shoulder ends. Too far down and the shirt looks borrowed. Too far in and the fabric pulls across your upper back. Shoulders are the hardest and most expensive thing to alter, so get them right at purchase.

Sleeves

A dress shirt cuff should land at the wrist bone and show roughly a quarter to half inch below a jacket sleeve. Casual sleeves can break the rule, but they should never swallow your hand.

Torso

You want clean lines, not vacuum sealing. Pinch two to three inches of fabric at the side of a shirt. Much more usually looks sloppy; much less usually looks strained when you sit.

Trousers

The waistband should stay in place without a belt. The seat should lie clean, and the front pockets should not flare. A slight break at the shoe is the easiest modern default.

Jacket length

A classic jacket covers most of the seat and visually divides your body in half. Fashion crops come and go. Balanced proportions keep working.

A $90 jacket that fits will beat a $900 jacket that doesn't.

THE FORMULA

Work: blue shirt + charcoal trousers + loafers + blazer.

Dinner: gray crewneck + dark denim + loafers.

Weekend: white T-shirt + chinos + clean sneakers.

COLOR

Build around four dependable neutrals.

Navy, gray, white and brown do most of the work. Olive, burgundy and light blue add variety without turning the closet into a puzzle.

SHOES

Own fewer pairs. Maintain them.

Start with one brown loafer or derby, one clean white leather sneaker and one weather-capable boot. Add black dress shoes only when your work or formal calendar actually demands them.

  • Use shoe trees after wear.
  • Rotate pairs instead of wearing one daily.
  • Wipe salt and dirt off before it sets.
  • Replace heels before the damage reaches the upper.

BUYING RULES

The dressing-room test.

  1. Can you name three outfits it improves?
  2. Does it fit today without a fantasy weight?
  3. Would you still buy it without the logo?
  4. Is the fabric appropriate for your actual life?
  5. Will you maintain it properly?
