Credit-Card Billing Error and Dispute File: Deadline, Evidence and Closure

A dispute button is not a complete case file. Identify the problem, anchor the federal deadline to the first statement that shows it, preserve copies, send notice through the issuer's stated route, and audit the account after the decision.

Published November 6, 2026. General U.S. consumer recordkeeping education, not legal or financial advice. Credit, debit, prepaid, installment and peer-to-peer payments can follow different rules; use the current account agreement, statement instructions and qualified legal help for fact-specific rights or court deadlines.

The useful answer

  • Start the federal credit-card billing-error clock from when the issuer sent the first periodic statement showing the error—not from the purchase date or the day you noticed it.
  • Call or use the issuer's online process promptly, but also follow the statement's written billing-inquiry instructions when you need to preserve Fair Credit Billing Act procedures.
  • Send copies, not originals, and keep the notice, enclosures, delivery proof, case number, contact chronology and every account adjustment in one file.
  • Keep paying undisputed amounts on time and reconcile any provisional credit without treating it as a final decision.
  • Separate billing error, product-quality, fraud, debit-card and identity-theft paths; the right evidence and deadline depend on the actual problem.

Classify the transaction before choosing the route

Capture the card type, issuer, last four digits, merchant descriptor, transaction date, posting date, amount and the first statement that displays it. Then describe the problem in one sentence: duplicate charge, wrong amount or date, missing return credit, payment not posted, goods not accepted or not delivered as agreed, request for clarification, or a transaction you did not authorize. The FTC identifies these as common credit-card billing-error categories, but accepted goods that merely disappoint on quality can follow a different claim path.

Do not call every problem a chargeback. Chargeback is commonly used for a network or issuer process, while federal billing-error rights have their own definitions, notice requirements and timelines. Ask the issuer what process is being opened and preserve the answer. If the transaction is on a debit card, prepaid card, bank transfer or peer-to-peer service, stop using this credit-card timeline as if it controls and obtain the specific institution and regulatory guidance.

An unauthorized charge can also signal identity theft or a compromised card. Contact the issuer immediately through a verified number, secure the account as directed and use IdentityTheft.gov when identity theft is suspected. Do not delay urgent containment while assembling a perfect packet.

  • Card and payment type: ________
  • First statement sent on: ________
  • Exact error category and amount: ________
  • Fraud or identity-theft containment needed: yes / no

Calculate and record the written-notice deadline

CFPB Regulation Z says a billing-error notice must reach the creditor at the disclosed billing-inquiry address no later than 60 days after the creditor transmitted the first periodic statement reflecting the alleged error. Record the statement date, add 60 calendar days, and set earlier drafting and delivery targets. Do not substitute the transaction date, expected-delivery date or an issuer app alert for the statement-transmission anchor unless qualified advice says another rule applies.

Use the statement's billing-rights instructions and the issuer's current agreement to find the correct notice destination. The billing-inquiry address can differ from the payment address. If the issuer expressly accepts electronic billing-error notices under its disclosed procedure, preserve the instructions and submission receipt. Otherwise, do not assume a chat message or merchant email satisfies the federal written-notice route.

Send early enough to document receipt. The FTC recommends certified mail with a return receipt when possible. Keep the signed letter, copies of enclosures, the mailing receipt, tracking result and delivered date. A calendar reminder should never be the only proof that notice was actually sent or received.

  • Statement-transmission date: ________
  • Day-60 date and earlier send-by date: ________
  • Issuer billing-inquiry address or accepted electronic route: ________
  • Delivery proof saved at: ________

Build a small evidence index

Create an index before sending attachments. Give each item a short identifier: statement page, order confirmation, receipt, return acceptance, tracking history, delivery promise, cancellation record, merchant correspondence, photograph or issuer message. List the date, source and what the item proves. Include only material evidence, redact unrelated account information where appropriate and never alter the original record.

For a missing delivery, distinguish the seller's promise, the carrier's scan and the actual delivery evidence. For a return credit, include the return authorization, carrier acceptance and the merchant's refund promise. For a duplicate or wrong amount, place the correct and disputed entries side by side. For an unauthorized transaction, state that clearly without inventing how the compromise occurred.

Keep originals in a protected folder and send copies. Export web chats and portal messages while access remains available. Screenshots should show enough context to identify the account or case without exposing full card numbers in a casually shared file. Record any merchant resolution, but do not let merchant back-and-forth consume the issuer deadline.

  • Evidence item, date and proposition: ________
  • Merchant contact and promised remedy: ________
  • Originals protected; copies prepared: yes / no
  • Sensitive data minimized before sharing: yes / no

Write a notice that is easy to investigate

Use a direct subject such as Notice of Billing Error and identify your name, address and account sufficiently for the issuer to locate it. State the disputed amount and transaction date, describe the error briefly, say what correction you request and list the enclosed copies. The FTC publishes a sample letter, but adapt it to the real facts rather than pasting allegations or legal conclusions that the documents do not support.

Ask for correction of the error and related finance or other charges where applicable, plus an accurate statement. Do not include passwords, PINs or a complete card number when the issuer does not require them. Sign and date the final notice, then save the exact transmitted version. If you call or file online too, record the date, representative or channel, case number and what was said without treating a phone promise as a substitute for written proof.

The CFPB says the issuer generally has 30 days to acknowledge a proper written notice unless it has completed the required resolution procedure in that period, and it must complete the investigation within two complete billing cycles, no more than 90 days. Track the actual notices received rather than predicting an outcome from silence.

  • Notice signed and dated: ________
  • Disputed amount, date and reason stated: yes / no
  • Requested correction and enclosure list stated: yes / no
  • Issuer case number and acknowledgment due: ________

Control payments and provisional credits during review

Continue reviewing every statement. CFPB guidance says you may withhold the disputed amount and related charges during a covered investigation, but you remain responsible for correct charges and undisputed amounts on time. Do not stop all payments, cancel autopay blindly or ignore the minimum-payment calculation. Confirm the issuer's instructions and route any uncertainty to qualified advice before a due date.

Label a temporary or provisional credit as provisional. Record when it posts, whether related interest or fees change, and whether the disputed transaction remains annotated. Do not spend on the assumption that the case is closed, and do not count both an issuer credit and a merchant refund as permanent. Regulation Z permits correction of duplicate credits when the consumer has received more than one credit for the same error.

If collection activity, a credit-reporting issue, account restriction or an unfamiliar request appears, preserve it and escalate through the issuer's written complaint process or the CFPB complaint route as appropriate. A court filing, threatened legal action or unresolved deadline is a reason to seek qualified consumer-law help, not to rely on a general web guide.

  • Undisputed balance and due date controlled: ________
  • Provisional credit date and amount: ________
  • Interest, fee and reporting status reviewed: ________
  • Escalation owner and deadline: ________

Close the case only after a statement audit

When a decision arrives, save the full written explanation. If the issuer finds an error, verify the disputed amount, related charges and account balance on the next statement. If it finds the bill correct, record its reason, the amount and payment date it states, and any document-request or appeal path. FTC guidance describes a written disagreement route after an adverse result; act within the specific stated time and obtain legal advice where the amount or consequences justify it.

Reconcile merchant and issuer credits so the account contains one correct resolution, not a hidden double credit that will be reversed later. Restore or revise autopay only after checking the new balance and due date. If the card number changed, update legitimate recurring charges through known merchant channels and monitor for follow-on misuse.

Close the file with a one-page outcome: issue, amount, notice and receipt dates, evidence index, issuer decision, final credits or charges, final statement checked and any identity-theft follow-up. Retain it under the household records policy while the account, tax, warranty, refund or legal need remains. The finished record should let another person reconstruct the case without guessing.

  • Written decision received and saved: ________
  • Final credit, fee and interest audit completed: ________
  • Merchant and issuer adjustments reconciled: ________
  • Case closed on and retained until: ________

Frequently asked questions

Is an online dispute enough for a credit-card billing error?

Use the issuer's disclosed procedure. CFPB and FTC guidance says a written billing-error notice sent to the designated address within the federal deadline protects statutory procedures; preserve any accepted electronic instructions and receipt.

Does the 60-day clock start on the purchase date?

For the Regulation Z billing-error notice, the key date is when the issuer transmitted the first periodic statement reflecting the alleged error. Record that statement date and send early.

Should I pay the disputed charge while the issuer investigates?

For a covered dispute, CFPB guidance says you may withhold the disputed amount and related charges, but correct and undisputed amounts remain due. Follow the issuer's instructions and seek advice when payment treatment is unclear.

What if the problem was on a debit card?

Do not assume credit-card rules and timing control a debit transaction. Contact the bank immediately and use the debit-card or electronic-fund-transfer procedure that applies to the account and facts.
