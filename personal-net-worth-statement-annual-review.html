Personal Net Worth Statement: Assets, Debts, Valuation Dates and Annual Review

Net worth is a dated balance-sheet calculation, not a verdict on your life and not a substitute for cash flow. Fix the owner, valuation date and evidence for every line so next year's comparison measures the same thing.

Published November 17, 2026. Independent educational worksheet, not financial, investment, tax, accounting, insurance, estate-planning or legal advice. It does not value a business, determine solvency, replace lender disclosures or measure complete financial well-being. Use current statements and qualified fiduciary, tax, accounting, appraisal, legal or benefits professionals when ownership, valuation, taxes, pensions, trusts, divorce, estates or business interests are material or uncertain.

The useful answer

  • Fix the person or household, ownership rules, currency and one as-of date before entering any number.
  • List assets and liabilities separately with an evidence source, valuation method and uncertainty note for each line.
  • Use defensible current values rather than purchase price, insured value, sentimental value or an optimistic future sale price.
  • Keep net worth separate from monthly cash flow, emergency liquidity, credit, goals and broader financial well-being.
  • Repeat the same method annually, explain major changes and preserve only the records needed for a secure review trail.

Freeze the scope before calculating

Investor.gov defines net worth as assets minus liabilities and recommends updating the statement regularly, such as yearly. That arithmetic is simple; the scope is where household statements go wrong. Write the legal owner or household, the included accounts, the currency and one as-of date. Decide how jointly owned assets and debts will appear. Do not switch between an individual view and a household view halfway through the page.

Create a statement ID and a comparison policy. A useful policy says that every line needs a source, date and valuation method, and that unresolved ownership appears in notes rather than being forced into a confident total. If a lender, court, fiduciary, benefit plan or tax authority requests a statement, use its definitions and professional guidance; this editorial worksheet is for personal annual review, not an official filing.

  • Statement owner or household: ________
  • As-of date, currency and statement ID: ________
  • Joint-ownership and inclusion policy: ________
  • Official filing or professional issue excluded: ________

Build the asset register from evidence

Group assets by how their value can be supported: cash and deposits, marketable investments, retirement accounts, real property, vehicles, business interests and other material property. Record the account or asset label without publishing full identifiers, the legal owner, evidence date, source, value and liquidity. Reconcile pending transactions so a transfer is not counted in both the sending and receiving account.

Do not inventory every household object merely to make the total larger. Ordinary clothing, furniture and electronics often have limited resale value and can add false precision. Include a material personal asset only when its ownership and realistic current value can be supported. Insurance replacement cost, tax assessment, asking price, appraisal, dealer offer and expected net sale proceeds answer different questions; label exactly which one you used.

  • Cash and deposit evidence: ________
  • Investment and retirement statement values: ________
  • Property, vehicle or business valuation method: ________
  • Liquidity, restrictions and uncertainty: ________

Value assets consistently and conservatively

For quoted securities, use the account value on the as-of date and preserve the statement. For a home, choose a repeatable documented source and note that gross estimated value is not sale proceeds after debt, taxes and transaction costs. For a vehicle, record the exact condition and valuation source. Closely held businesses, pensions, annuities, trusts, options and restricted interests may require professional valuation or a separate note rather than a guessed number.

Keep basis and tax consequences out of the headline number unless a qualified method explicitly incorporates them, but flag them where they could materially change spendable value. The point is not to make every asset perfectly liquid. It is to prevent unlike values from masquerading as equal. A dollar in a transaction account, a restricted retirement account and an uncertain private-business estimate can appear in the same asset total only when their liquidity and uncertainty remain visible.

  • Valuation source and retrieval date: ________
  • Gross value versus estimated net proceeds: ________
  • Restrictions, tax or liquidity note: ________
  • Professional valuation needed: yes / no / why

Capture every liability on the same date

List mortgage and home-equity balances, vehicle and student loans, personal loans, credit cards, taxes due, medical balances and other material obligations. Use the current outstanding or payoff amount appropriate to the purpose and label which one it is. Record creditor, responsible borrower, balance date, interest-rate type, maturity or minimum payment when useful, and whether the debt is secured by an asset already listed.

A credit limit is not a liability, and the monthly payment is not the balance. Avoid subtracting a mortgage from the house line and then listing it again in liabilities. Note disputed, contingent, co-signed, guaranteed or unknown obligations separately and seek qualified help when legal responsibility is unclear. Pending card charges, accrued interest and tax liabilities can make a statement stale quickly; reconcile material items before calling the total final.

  • Creditor, borrower and debt type: ________
  • Outstanding or payoff balance and date: ________
  • Secured asset or guarantee link: ________
  • Disputed, contingent or uncertain obligation: ________

Calculate without double counting

Total the documented asset lines, total the documented liability lines, then subtract liabilities from assets. Add a reconciliation check: every balance should tie to one source, every transfer should land once, and every secured debt should remain a separate liability. Do not add the value of an investment account and then add each holding again, or count an escrow balance already included elsewhere.

Preserve subtotals by liquidity and uncertainty instead of displaying only one dramatic number. Suggested fields are liquid assets, long-term or restricted assets, other documented assets, short-term liabilities and long-term liabilities. The arithmetic total may be negative, zero or positive; none of those results alone diagnoses financial health. Treat the output as a dated inventory that supports better questions, not a status ranking.

  • Documented assets total: ________
  • Documented liabilities total: ________
  • Assets minus liabilities: ________
  • Reconciliation and double-count check completed: ________

Keep cash flow and financial well-being separate

Investor.gov treats the net-worth statement and the record of income and expenses as related but separate tools. A household can have substantial net worth and poor monthly liquidity, or modest net worth and a stable cash surplus. Maintain a separate cash-flow view for income timing, required bills, variable spending and debt payments. Maintain a separate emergency-access view for funds available without selling, borrowing or creating a tax or penalty event.

CFPB likewise cautions that financial well-being cannot be reduced to measures such as income, credit score or net worth. Its framework includes control over day-to-day finances, capacity to absorb a shock, progress toward goals and freedom of choice. Use the net-worth statement to identify ownership, concentration, debts and documentation gaps, then connect those findings to goals and qualified advice. Do not turn one annual delta into an investment recommendation.

  • Monthly cash-flow record location: ________
  • Emergency-access amount and constraints: ________
  • Concentration or documentation issue: ________
  • Goal or qualified-advice question: ________

Run the annual review and secure the file

Repeat the statement on the same calendar anchor and, where practical, the same valuation sources. Copy the prior line structure, then verify ownership, beneficiaries where relevant, account status, debt balances and evidence dates. Explain major changes as contributions, withdrawals, market movement, debt repayment, purchases, sales, transfers, inheritance, gifts, reclassification or corrected data. A written bridge is more useful than celebrating or fearing the percentage change alone.

Store the working file and evidence index with access appropriate to its sensitivity. Avoid placing full account numbers, passwords or authentication secrets in the worksheet. Record where official statements are held, who can access the annual file in an emergency, and the retention or destruction rule. End with dated actions: correct one missing document, resolve one unknown obligation, update one beneficiary or ownership question through the proper channel, and schedule the next review.

  • Major change and evidence bridge: ________
  • Secure storage, access and retention rule: ________
  • Next actions, owners and review date: ________

Frequently asked questions

What is personal net worth?

It is the value of documented assets minus documented liabilities for a defined person or household on a stated date. The scope, ownership and valuation method should accompany the number.

Should my home be included in net worth?

It can be included when ownership, a defensible current value and the associated mortgage are recorded consistently. Label gross estimated value separately from possible net sale proceeds and disclose uncertainty.

Do retirement accounts count as net worth?

They are commonly included as assets, but restrictions, taxes, penalties, vesting and plan rules can affect accessibility. Use the current statement value and keep liquidity limitations visible.

How often should I calculate net worth?

An annual review on a consistent date is a practical baseline. Update sooner after a major ownership, debt, estate or household event, while avoiding noisy conclusions from small short-term market moves.
