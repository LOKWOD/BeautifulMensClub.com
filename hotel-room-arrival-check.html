The Hotel Room Arrival Check: Safety, Bedbugs, Sleep and Checkout

Do not unpack on arrival. Confirm the room, trace the exits, inspect the sleep area and fix problems while the front desk can still move you.

Published September 9, 2026. General travel-planning guidance. Property conditions, laws and emergency procedures vary; follow staff and emergency-service instructions during an incident.

The useful answer

  • Keep the reservation confirmation and verify the room type, dates, included benefits and payment terms before settling in.
  • Read the evacuation map, locate two exits, count doors to the stairs and identify the nearest alarm pull station.
  • Keep luggage off the bed while checking mattress seams, bedding and the headboard area for bedbug evidence.
  • Test the lock, lights, thermostat, water, outlets and any accessibility features you depend on while a room change is still practical.
  • Photograph or document a material problem, report it promptly and get any room, rate or fee resolution in writing.

Before the key works: verify the transaction

Save the original confirmation where it remains available without hotel Wi-Fi. It should show the property, address, dates, room type, occupancy, cancellation terms, taxes, mandatory fees, deposits, parking and included benefits. If a third-party booking is involved, know which company controls changes and payment. A verbal promise at a call center is harder to resolve than a written confirmation number and timestamp.

The Federal Trade Commission warns that scam travel sites and phishing messages can imitate recognizable hotels or airlines. Reach the property through a number or web address you independently verify when a message asks for new payment details, a wire, gift card or cryptocurrency. Do not use the contact route supplied by a suspicious text simply because it includes your travel dates.

At check-in, avoid saying a room number aloud when discretion matters, and ask staff to write it instead. Confirm checkout time, parking access, breakfast or lounge terms, incidental authorization and how the deposit is released. For accessibility, allergy or medical-equipment needs, inspect the actual assigned room rather than assuming a reservation note guarantees that every required feature is present.

Minute one: build the fire exit in your head

The U.S. Fire Administration advises hotel guests to read the evacuation plan, find the two closest exits, count the doors between the room and those exits and locate fire alarms on the floor. Do this before fatigue, darkness or smoke makes navigation difficult. Walk the primary route if practical and confirm that the stair door opens from the corridor without obstruction.

Look at where the room sits relative to elevators, stairs and building wings. Keep the room key, phone and shoes in a consistent bedside location, but never delay evacuation to collect luggage. If an alarm sounds or staff or emergency responders give instructions, follow them. Do not assume an elevator is a safe exit during a fire unless emergency personnel explicitly direct its use.

USFA's hotel-motel master list identifies properties meeting specified smoke-alarm and sprinkler criteria for federal travelers, but no list replaces attention on arrival. Report a blocked corridor, damaged exit sign, disabled alarm or door that will not operate. If the immediate condition feels unsafe, leave the area and contact emergency services rather than negotiating from inside it.

Minute three: keep the suitcase off the bed

Set luggage on a hard surface, in the bathroom or on an inspected luggage rack while checking the sleeping area. The CDC says bedbugs occur in hotels across quality levels and that cleanliness does not determine whether they are present. They can hide in luggage seams, mattress folds, box springs, bed frames, headboards, furniture and cracks.

Use a flashlight and inspect visible mattress and sheet folds, the accessible headboard area and nearby seams without dismantling hotel property. CDC-listed clues include live bugs, shed exoskeletons, rusty-colored blood spots and a sweet musty odor. Bite marks alone are not a reliable arrival test and can appear later or resemble other bites.

If you find credible signs, stop unpacking, take clear photographs and contact management. Ask for the property's response and a different room or accommodation as appropriate; moving next door without a plan may not be a meaningful remedy. Keep belongings contained until you have guidance. Do not spray your own pesticide into a hotel room or place unknown chemicals on bedding.

Minute six: test the room while a move is easy

Close and open the entry door, engage the deadbolt and secondary latch, and confirm that the door self-closes and latches. Check connecting doors from your side. Report a damaged lock rather than improvising a device that could obstruct emergency exit. Use the peephole or communicate through the closed door, and verify an unexpected worker by calling the front desk through an independently confirmed number.

Test the lights, thermostat, water, toilet, outlets needed for medical or work equipment and the room phone if it is part of your emergency plan. Confirm that windows and balcony doors operate only as intended and that child-safety concerns are addressed for the travelers in the room. Never defeat a smoke alarm or block a sprinkler because of nuisance light, sound or placement.

For an accessible room, test the features that matter to the guest: route clearance, door operation, bathroom access, transfer space, controls, alarms and any communication device requested. Report a mismatch immediately and state the functional requirement, not only the reservation category. Photographing the issue can help the desk understand what must change.

Minute eight: set up sleep and privacy

Check noise, curtain closure, bedding, room temperature and the alarm clock before unpacking. Disable an unwanted previous alarm and set your own backup. Request a room change early when elevator noise, a mechanical fault, smoke odor or another condition makes sleep unlikely. A late-night complaint is harder to solve when the property is full.

Put medication, identification, keys and essential travel documents in one deliberate location. A room safe can be a useful layer but is not a universal guarantee; follow its instructions and keep a separate record of essential numbers. Do not leave a passport or medicine loose in bedding, towels or drawers where it can be forgotten during a hurried checkout.

Use the do-not-disturb control appropriately, lock the door when occupied and do not share the room number casually. If someone calls claiming to be the front desk and asks for payment information, hang up and contact the desk using the room phone directory or a number you verify independently. Urgency plus a new payment method is a reason to slow down.

Checkout begins the night before

Review the folio in the app, television or front desk before the departure rush when possible. Compare the nightly rate, taxes, parking, destination or resort fees, food charges and deposit treatment with the confirmation and signed terms. Ask about an unfamiliar charge while staff can investigate it, and obtain any agreed correction in writing.

Create a fixed sweep: outlets and chargers, bathroom, shower hooks, closet, safe, drawers, under bedding, behind curtains and the luggage-rack area. Keep clothing contained rather than shaking it around the room if bedbug evidence appeared during the stay. If you later suspect exposure, use qualified pest-control or public-health guidance instead of bringing an unsealed suitcase straight into the bedroom.

Save the final receipt and note unresolved issues, names and reference numbers. Report serious safety or accessibility problems through the property's formal channel and the relevant local authority when appropriate. The arrival check is successful when it turns a vague room into a known system: transaction verified, two exits understood, sleep area inspected, essential functions tested and checkout evidence preserved.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check first in a hotel room?

Verify the room and reservation, then read the evacuation map and locate two exits before unpacking. Keep luggage off the bed while inspecting the sleep area.

Does a clean hotel room mean there cannot be bedbugs?

No. The CDC says bedbugs can occur in hotels at any quality level and cleanliness does not determine whether they are present.

What signs of bedbugs should I look for?

CDC guidance lists live bugs, shed skins, rusty-colored spots and a sweet musty odor. Check visible mattress and sheet folds, the headboard area and nearby seams without dismantling the room.

Why count doors to the stairwell?

The U.S. Fire Administration recommends it because smoke or darkness can make exit signs and landmarks harder to see during an emergency.
