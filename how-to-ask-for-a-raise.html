How to Ask for a Raise: Evidence, Timing, Number and Follow-Up

A raise conversation is not a verdict on your worth. It is a business request about role, results, market, budget and timing—stronger when each part is documented.

Published September 22, 2026. General career and communication guidance, not legal, tax or individualized employment advice. Contracts, collective bargaining, pay transparency and discrimination rights vary by role and jurisdiction.

The useful answer

  • Build a one-page evidence file connecting expanded scope and measurable results to the organization's priorities; attendance and effort alone are not the whole case.
  • Use several relevant compensation sources and match occupation, level, geography, industry, schedule and total compensation before quoting a range.
  • Choose a specific request and a defensible range, then schedule a real conversation instead of dropping the number into an unrelated meeting.
  • Ask directly, pause, and learn the decision process; do not bluff another offer, threaten departure or turn colleagues' private pay into gossip.
  • End with a written record of the decision, criteria, owner and date. If pay concerns involve unlawful discrimination or retaliation, use qualified and timely support.

Separate the compensation question from self-worth

A compensation decision can reflect budget, pay bands, internal equity, timing, role scope and management judgment. It does not produce a complete measure of your value as a person. That separation helps you prepare a specific business request and hear a no without collapsing the conversation into praise, loyalty or grievance.

Define the decision you want: an adjustment to base salary, a promotion with a new band, correction of an apparent pay inconsistency, a bonus, equity, schedule change, title change or another form of compensation. These are related but not interchangeable. Asking for everything at once can hide the main request and make the answer impossible to record.

Review the employment agreement, handbook, collective-bargaining terms, review cycle and applicable pay-transparency rules before choosing the route. Some organizations use formal windows or job-reclassification processes. A manager may recommend a raise without having authority to approve it, so learn who decides and what documentation the system requires.

Build the one-page evidence file

List the role you were hired to do and the scope you now carry. Add projects delivered, revenue or cost impact where genuinely attributable, risk reduced, quality or cycle-time improvements, customers retained, systems built, people trained and responsibilities added. Use dates, baselines and sources. Replace helped improve with the part you actually owned and the observed result.

Connect each item to a current organizational priority. A long accomplishment inventory is less useful than three or four examples that show sustained higher-level contribution. Distinguish team outcomes from individual work without stealing credit. Name collaborators and your role accurately; credibility is more valuable than an inflated claim that a manager can disprove.

Add the next-period case. Describe the scope you are already sustaining and the work you can responsibly own next, but do not promise impossible output in exchange for ordinary pay. The evidence file should fit on one page with links or backup details available. Its job is to support a decision, not overwhelm the meeting with a personal annual report.

Use market data without pretending it is a price tag

Start with reliable public data. The Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics program publishes annual estimates by occupation, geography and industry, while the Occupational Outlook Handbook adds role context. Those broad datasets are useful anchors, not automatic quotations for one employee with a particular level, specialty, employer and total package.

Compare like with like: job content, seniority, location, industry, organization size, schedule, remote status, hours, credentials and supervisory scope. A title can hide several occupations. Use more than one source when possible, record each source's date and methodology, and avoid combining base salary, bonus and equity into one mystery number.

Internal information also needs care. Published pay bands, your prior offers and documented role ranges are stronger than rumors. Do not identify a colleague's private compensation without permission or build the case around who deserves less. The question is whether your compensation matches your documented work and the employer's lawful, consistent system.

Choose the request before the meeting

Select a primary number or percentage, the evidence supporting it and a range you would regard as meaningful. Check whether you are discussing base salary, hourly rate, total cash or total compensation. A precise request is easier to evaluate than I was hoping for more. Do not manufacture mathematical precision from weak market data; explain the range and the judgment used.

Choose timing when evidence is current and a decision can still affect planning. Strong moments can include completion of sustained expanded scope, a formal review or the budget cycle—provided the organization's process allows it. Do not ambush a manager during a crisis, a public event or another person's meeting. Ask for a dedicated conversation with a clear subject.

Use a short invitation: I would like to discuss my role scope and compensation. Could we set aside thirty minutes next week? If policy requires a form or advance memo, follow it. The calendar request should not contain the entire argument, and it should not imply that a routine check-in is secretly an ultimatum.

Make the case, ask, then stop talking

Open with the role and evidence, not an apology: Over the past year I took responsibility for X and Y, and the documented results were A and B. Based on that scope and the market context in this summary, I am requesting that my base salary move to __. Then pause. A clear silence gives the other person room to respond.

Answer questions with the record. If a result is estimated, label it. If the manager disputes scope or attribution, ask which fact or standard differs and note it for follow-up. Do not introduce rent, debt or another person's lifestyle as the employer's business rationale. Personal needs are real, but the raise case is more portable when grounded in role and compensation structure.

Avoid counterfeit leverage. Do not claim another offer, deadline or intent to leave unless it is true and you are prepared for the consequences. Do not threaten disclosure, resign in the room as theater or criticize peers. Firmness is compatible with professionalism: you can state what adjustment you seek and later decide whether the answer works for you.

Turn no, not now and maybe into usable information

If the answer is no, ask which constraint controls: budget, band, performance, tenure, timing, approval authority or role classification. Then ask what evidence or scope would support reconsideration, who owns the next decision and when it will occur. A vague work harder answer is not a plan; request observable criteria without arguing every sentence in the meeting.

For not now, put a date on now. Agree on two or three measurable conditions and schedule the review before leaving the topic. If the manager cannot name a date or criteria, treat that uncertainty as information for your career plan. Continue performing responsibly, but do not convert an indefinite possibility into guaranteed future income.

If base pay cannot move, discuss only alternatives that matter to you and that the employer can document: bonus, equity, title and scope, schedule, paid time, professional development or a promotion pathway. Understand tax, vesting, repayment and eligibility terms before agreeing. A one-time payment does not permanently raise base compensation.

Document the outcome without making it adversarial

Send a concise follow-up: Thank you for discussing my compensation request. My understanding is that __, the decision owner is __, the criteria are __, and we will review this on __. Please correct anything I captured inaccurately. This creates a shared operating record rather than a courtroom transcript.

Save the evidence file, sources, meeting date, written response and later changes to scope. Do not record a conversation secretly without understanding applicable law and policy. Keep confidential material in approved systems, and do not remove proprietary or customer data to build a personal portfolio.

If the request is approved, confirm effective date, new base or rate, title, bonus treatment, benefits effects and when the change will appear in payroll. Review the actual statement. If it is declined, decide on a reasonable review period and your alternatives. Continued waiting is a choice that should be made with dates and evidence.

Recognize when the issue is not negotiation

Pay differences can involve unlawful discrimination, retaliation, wage-and-hour violations or contract issues. The EEOC explains that federal compensation protections can cover salary, overtime, bonuses, stock options, benefits and other forms of pay, with different coverage and time limits under different laws. Do not assume a generic negotiation script preserves a legal deadline.

If you suspect discrimination or another violation, keep factual records and consult the appropriate union representative, agency or qualified employment lawyer promptly. Avoid copying material you are not authorized to possess. Emergency advice from coworkers or social media is not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific guidance.

For an ordinary business disagreement, use the same record to decide whether to stay, seek an internal move or conduct an external search. You do not need to announce every option. The best outcome is not always winning the meeting; it is obtaining enough clear information to make the next decision deliberately.

Frequently asked questions

How much of a raise should I ask for?

Use role scope, internal bands and several relevant market sources to choose a specific, defensible request. There is no universal percentage that fits every promotion, correction or employer.

Should I mention another job offer?

Only if the offer is real, you may ethically disclose it and you are prepared for the employer's response. A fake offer is risky and unnecessary.

What if my manager says there is no budget?

Ask whether the constraint is timing or the role's range, who controls the next decision, what alternatives exist and the exact date for review. Record the answer and assess it realistically.

Can I compare my pay with a coworker's?

Use lawful, accurate information carefully, but do not expose a colleague's private details or make the case about who should earn less. Pay-discrimination concerns may require timely, qualified guidance.
