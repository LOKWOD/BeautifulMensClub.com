THE PRINCIPLES

Train. Recover.
Repeat for years.

Consistency beats novelty. Progress comes from doing a small number of useful movements with better control, more load or more repetitions over time.

You do not need to leave every workout destroyed. You need to leave capable of coming back.

“The best program is the one you can still do when work gets ugly.”

POSTURE

Stop trying to “stand up straight.”

Build the upper back, train the glutes, improve hip mobility and take breaks from the chair. Posture is a capacity problem more than a motivational speech.

  • Rows or pulldowns every session.
  • Loaded carries twice a week.
  • Five-minute walk each hour.
  • Screen near eye level.

CONDITIONING

Keep an easy gear.

Two or three weekly sessions of brisk walking, cycling or incline treadmill work build an aerobic base without wrecking recovery. You should be able to speak in short sentences.

RECOVERY

Protect the boring things.

Regular sleep, enough protein, hydration and one truly easy day do more for progress than another supplement or complicated “biohack.”

SLEEP

Keep a consistent wake time whenever possible.

FOOD

Build meals around protein, produce and a useful carb.

LOG

Track sets, reps and load. Memory is not a program.
