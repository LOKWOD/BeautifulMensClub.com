Recurring Subscription Audit and Cancellation File: Find, Decide, Cancel and Prove

A clean subscription list is not a screenshot of logos. It is a control file that identifies the contracting company, the real billing route, the next charge, the cancellation method and the evidence that proves what happened after you pressed the button.

Published November 14, 2026. General U.S. consumer education, not legal, financial, tax or contract advice. Subscription, automatic-renewal and cancellation rules vary by product, payment rail and state. The FTC's 2024 amended Click-to-Cancel rule was vacated by a federal court in July 2025, and the agency restored the prior federal rule text in 2026; verify current federal, state, contract and issuer requirements before relying on a deadline or remedy.

The useful answer

  • Inventory from bank and card statements, app stores, email receipts and household devices; a visible app icon is neither a complete billing list nor proof of cancellation.
  • Separate the service contract from the payment authorization: stopping an automatic debit may not cancel the underlying agreement or amount owed.
  • Record the merchant, plan, billing route, price, renewal date, household owner, use and cancellation method before deciding to keep, downgrade or cancel.
  • Preserve the terms, request, confirmation, timestamp and post-cancellation statement; a cancellation screen without the account and effective date is weak evidence.
  • Escalate a continuing charge through the merchant and the correct bank, card or platform process promptly, using current official instructions rather than a generic script.

Build the inventory from money leaving the house

Review at least the prior twelve months of credit-card, debit-card and bank statements so annual renewals are not missed. Search email for receipt, renewal, trial, membership and subscription; review Apple, Google and other platform subscription pages; check PayPal or similar approved-payment lists; and ask household members which services they control. Do not store full card or bank numbers in the worksheet. A last-four identifier and institution name are usually enough to route the record.

Normalize each line to the company that actually contracts and bills, not just the product name seen on a screen. Record any statement descriptor, platform intermediary, household account email, plan name, charge amount and frequency. A streaming add-on billed through a device platform, a newspaper billed directly and a gym drafted from a bank account require different cancellation and dispute paths even when all appear monthly.

  • Merchant and statement descriptor: ________
  • Direct / app store / payment platform / bank debit: ________
  • Household account owner and login route: ________
  • Monthly, annual and irregular total: ________

Recover the controlling terms before judging value

Save the current plan page, original confirmation if available, price-change or renewal notice, minimum term, trial conversion date, renewal date, cancellation route and any notice period. FTC guidance advises reading the details of free trials and auto-renewals, checking expected price and knowing how to cancel. Do not rely on memory of an introductory price after the plan, bundle or billing interval has changed.

Identify what cancellation actually changes: immediate loss of access, service through the paid period, a downgrade at the next renewal, an early-termination amount, return of rented equipment or deletion of stored data. Separate a contractual obligation from a payment method. CFPB guidance states that canceling an automatic payment does not by itself cancel the underlying service contract or debt.

  • Terms and renewal notice saved on: ________
  • Trial, price change, renewal and notice dates: ________
  • Access, equipment, data and refund consequences: ________
  • Contract cancellation versus payment change: ________

Use a decision test that survives a promotion

Assign one owner and one job to every subscription. Record actual use over the previous billing period, the people who depend on it, a credible substitute, switching friction and the cost at the next nonpromotional rate. Keep can be a sound decision; the audit is not a contest to cancel the most. The goal is to eliminate forgotten, duplicated or poorly controlled charges and to make intentional services visible.

Compare downgrade, pause, annual and bundle offers only after the current plan passes the job test. Annual billing can reduce a nominal monthly rate while increasing prepayment risk and the cost of a wrong decision. A retention discount may restart a minimum term or change cancellation rights. Save the new terms and mark the next full-price date instead of treating a chat promise as permanent.

  • Service job and household owner: ________
  • Use in the last billing period: ________
  • Keep / downgrade / pause / cancel with reason: ________
  • Next full-price date and review reminder: ________

Cancel through the route that controls the account

Start with the official account or platform that shows the subscription and follow its current instructions. Confirm that the account, plan and billing source match the inventory row before submitting. If a service was purchased through an app store or payment platform, a direct merchant login may not control the renewal. Avoid links in an unexpected renewal message; reach the company through a known site or app because fake notices can seek payment credentials.

State the requested outcome precisely: cancel renewal, terminate now, end at the paid-through date, remove an add-on or revoke a separate automatic debit. Record the date, local time, route, representative or case number, exact wording, effective date, final charge, refund position and equipment-return instructions. Do not threaten, guess at law or erase the account while a receipt, export or dispute record is still needed.

  • Official cancellation route and account matched: ________
  • Exact requested outcome and effective date: ________
  • Case number, representative or submission ID: ________
  • Final charge, refund and equipment return: ________

Preserve proof that can answer a later charge

Capture the relevant terms before submission, the completed cancellation page, confirmation email or chat transcript and the account status afterward. Include the service and account identifier, request date, effective date and confirmation number in the same folder. FTC consumer guidance specifically recommends keeping the cancellation request and notes of conversations and monitoring statements for charges after cancellation.

A screenshot is strongest when its context is clear. Name files with date, merchant and event; preserve the full email headers or original downloadable receipt where practical; and write a short chronology instead of relying on a pile of images. Keep proof long enough to cover the final statement, promised refund, chargeback window, equipment receipt and any reasonable contract or tax record need.

  • Submitted request and confirmation file: ________
  • Account status and paid-through date: ________
  • Final statement, refund or return proof: ________

Route automatic-payment control correctly

For a company pulling automatic payments from a U.S. bank account, CFPB guidance says a consumer can tell the company that authorization is revoked and tell the bank or credit union, following up in writing or email. A bank may also use a stop-payment order and may charge a fee. Follow the institution's current process and timing. Revoking a debit is not permission to ignore a valid contract, loan or final bill.

Credit-card, debit-card, bank-transfer and platform disputes have different rights, evidence and time limits. Contact the merchant first when appropriate, then use the issuer or bank route tied to the actual transaction and act promptly. If a business will not stop billing after a documented cancellation, preserve every statement and response. Use current FTC, CFPB, issuer and state-attorney-general instructions; this worksheet does not determine whether a particular charge is legally unauthorized.

  • Merchant contract canceled separately: yes / no / not applicable
  • Authorization revocation or stop-payment route: ________
  • Issuer, bank, platform or regulator follow-up: ________

Close the file only after the money agrees

Check the next two statements or the period appropriate to the billing interval. Confirm that no renewal posted, a promised refund arrived, any final amount matches the written terms and rented equipment was accepted. Remove saved payment credentials only when that action will not destroy needed proof or interfere with another valid service. Update the household bill calendar and annual total so the next audit starts from a clean baseline.

If a charge continues, reopen the chronology with the new transaction date, amount and descriptor. Do not create repeated duplicate disputes without reading the institution's response. Escalate unresolved contract interpretation, large losses, identity theft, debt collection or state-law questions to the appropriate agency or qualified professional. Close with an outcome, date, evidence location and next review—not merely the feeling that the subscription is probably gone.

Frequently asked questions

Does deleting an app cancel its subscription?

Usually not. Find the account or platform that controls billing and follow its current cancellation instructions; preserve confirmation and check the next statement.

Does stopping an automatic bank payment cancel the service contract?

No. CFPB guidance warns that stopping an automatic payment does not itself cancel the underlying contract or amount owed. Address the merchant agreement separately.

Is the federal Click-to-Cancel rule currently in force?

The FTC's 2024 amended rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025, and the FTC restored the prior rule text in 2026. Other federal and state laws may apply, so verify current official guidance for the transaction.

How long should I keep cancellation proof?

Keep it through the final statement, refund, equipment return and any applicable dispute or contract period. Longer retention may be appropriate when a charge, tax record, debt or legal issue remains open.
