Home Electrical Panel Circuit Directory and Emergency Shutoff File

A handwritten word beside a breaker is not an emergency plan. Build a room-and-load directory from ordinary controls only, preserve every unknown instead of guessing, and give the household one clear handoff for outages, urgent shutoffs and licensed service.

Published November 12, 2026. General U.S. home-safety organization, not electrical, emergency, code or insurance advice. Never remove a panel cover or dead front, touch internal components, work on damaged or wet equipment, or test beyond normal user-operated controls. Use the utility, emergency services and a qualified licensed electrician for the specific property and jurisdiction.

The useful answer

  • Work only from the closed panel face and normal switches, plugs and appliance controls; the directory does not justify opening electrical equipment.
  • Record panel identity, location, service relationship and every label exactly before changing the directory.
  • Verify one branch circuit at a time with a second adult, a written test route and a stop rule for heat, odor, noise, arcing, damage or uncertainty.
  • Name circuits by rooms, fixed loads and exceptions; never turn an unverified guess into a clean printed label.
  • Keep an emergency handoff beside the household plan, not over required warnings or ventilation, and update it after every qualified electrical change.

Set the boundary before making the map

This file is a documentation project, not an electrical repair. The allowed work is looking at the closed panel face, reading existing labels and using ordinary switches, receptacle-connected lamps and appliance controls as they are normally used. Do not remove the panel cover or dead front, insert a tool, defeat an interlock, expose conductors, tighten hardware or improvise a tester. A directory can reduce confusion; it cannot make energized work safe.

Stop before testing if the panel is wet, corroded, hot, buzzing, crackling, smoking, visibly damaged, missing covers, giving shocks, showing scorch marks or surrounded by a burning odor. CPSC identifies frequent trips, flickering or dimming, hot or discolored wall plates, odors, buzzing and shocks as warning signs that need attention. Keep people away, use emergency services for fire or immediate danger, use the utility for service-side emergencies, and use a licensed electrician for diagnosis.

  • Closed panel face only; no cover removal: confirmed
  • Dry, intact, quiet and cool equipment: yes / stop
  • Emergency, utility and electrician contacts: ________
  • Household member who can explain the file: ________

Create the panel identity record

Start with a wide photo of the closed panel in its surroundings and a straight-on photo of the existing directory and breaker handles. Record the building address, unit, panel location, date, panel nickname, manufacturer and model only where those details are visible without opening anything. Note whether another subpanel, disconnect or meter location is known, but label that relationship as unverified until a qualified person confirms it.

Copy every existing circuit label exactly, including misspellings and blanks, into a baseline column. Do not erase the old record during discovery. Photograph required warnings and keep the finished directory from covering them, the latch, ventilation or access space. If a label names a former room or tenant, preserve the old wording in the history while testing what the breaker currently controls.

  • Address / unit / panel location: ________
  • Visible maker, model and panel nickname: ________
  • Baseline photos and label transcription saved: ________
  • Other panel, disconnect or service relationship: known / unverified

Plan a two-person verification route

Use two adults when practical: one remains at the closed panel and operates only the normal breaker handle; the other checks a written route of lights, ordinary receptacle-connected lamps and fixed equipment controls. Keep phones or another communication method available. Tell everyone in the home that a mapping session is underway, and postpone it if someone depends on medical equipment, remote work, security systems, refrigeration, pumps or another load that must not be interrupted.

Before switching anything, list sensitive equipment and use its manufacturer shutdown process. Do not unplug or cycle equipment you do not understand. Do not use a noncontact voltage detector, receptacle tester or meter as a shortcut unless a qualified person has taught the exact device and task; those tools have limits and can create false confidence. This guide does not teach electrical testing.

  • Panel operator / room checker: ________
  • Rooms and fixed loads in test order: ________
  • Sensitive, medical, network and security loads protected: ________
  • Communication and immediate stop word: ________

Verify one branch circuit without guessing

Choose one clearly identified branch breaker, record its visible identifier and existing label, and note the time. With the room checker ready, use the breaker handle as designed and observe which already-on lights or ordinary plugged lamps lose power. Check the planned rooms, hallways, exterior points and nearby exceptions. Restore the handle to its normal position when the observation is complete. Do not repeatedly cycle a breaker or reset one that trips during normal use.

Record positive observations and limitations. A receptacle that had nothing operating from it is not verified. A light going dark does not prove every outlet in the room is on the same circuit. Multiwire arrangements, shared equipment and local wiring can be more complex than a room label suggests. Mark an ambiguity for a licensed electrician rather than inferring the hidden wiring.

  • Visible breaker identifier and old label: ________
  • Lights, receptacle-connected lamps and fixed loads observed off: ________
  • Areas checked with no result: ________
  • Unknown, shared or contradictory result: ________

Write labels that survive a real emergency

Use a stable room name plus a specific load or boundary: 'Kitchen—counter receptacles, north wall' is more useful than 'plugs,' and 'Bedroom 2 + hall light' is better than a family member's name. Name fixed appliances only after verification. Preserve separate rows when two breakers serve one room, and state exceptions when one circuit crosses floors or rooms. Keep abbreviations defined in the handoff file.

Do not label a breaker 'spare' because it produced no result in one pass. It may serve an outdoor point, alarm, attic, pump, future wiring or equipment that was off. Use 'no load identified—do not assume spare' and escalate. Print a legible directory sized for the holder, keep a dated full copy in the household emergency file, and never paste over manufacturer information or required markings.

  • Stable room or zone name: ________
  • Specific load, wall or boundary: ________
  • Cross-room exception and abbreviation key: ________
  • Unidentified circuit preserved as unknown: yes / no

Build the emergency shutoff handoff

The handoff should answer who calls 911, who calls the utility, where occupants meet, how to reach a licensed electrician and where the directory is stored. Record only shutoff controls that are plainly identified and that the household has been instructed to operate. Do not tell an untrained person to open a panel, enter a flooded area or approach equipment that is hot, arcing, smoking or damaged.

Ready.gov advises turning off utilities only when appropriate and following local official or utility instructions. After a flood or major water event, keep away from wet electrical equipment and have the property assessed by qualified professionals. After a fire, shock, suspected arc or service damage, the emergency plan takes priority over saving appliances or completing a directory.

  • 911 / utility emergency / electrician contacts: ________
  • Safe household meeting place: ________
  • Directory copy and main emergency file location: ________
  • Flood, fire, shock and damaged-equipment stop language included: yes / no

Turn warning signs into a service record

Create a log for trips, flicker, dimming, odors, sound, heat, shocks, discoloration and damaged cords or outlets. Record the date, time, room, equipment in use and what the occupants observed without attempting to reproduce the event. Frequent breaker trips are protective signals, not an invitation to install a larger breaker or hold a handle in place. Never change breaker size or bypass protection to keep a load running.

Give the electrician the directory, baseline photo, event log and list of unknowns. Ask the professional to correct the circuit schedule after approved work and to explain any labels that depend on equipment or local terminology. Keep invoices, permits and inspection records with the panel history where applicable. The useful outcome is not a flawless amateur map; it is a reliable handoff that makes qualified work faster and household decisions safer.

  • Date, location and exact observation: ________
  • Loads operating before the event: ________
  • Emergency, utility or electrician response: ________
  • Corrected label, permit or inspection record attached: ________

Audit the directory after every change

Review the directory after renovations, appliance changes, a panel or service upgrade, adding backup power, a new tenant or any professional correction. Date every version. Do not silently copy labels from an old panel to replacement equipment. A generator transfer system, solar installation, battery or other power source changes the emergency picture and requires system-specific instructions from qualified installers.

Run a household read-through twice a year with the smoke-alarm, outage and emergency-document checks. Confirm that the panel remains accessible, required clearances and warnings are unobstructed, contact information works and the printed directory matches the digital copy. Do not store flammable supplies or household clutter around the panel. Close the audit with unresolved items assigned to a person and a date rather than declaring the system complete.

  • Renovation, appliance or power-system change: ________

  • Open questions, owner and due date: ________

Frequently asked questions

Can I remove the panel cover to trace a circuit?

No. This worksheet stays outside the closed panel and does not teach energized work. Use a licensed electrician for hidden wiring, internal inspection or any uncertainty.

Should I reset a breaker that keeps tripping?

Do not repeatedly reset it or defeat protection. Record the load and circumstances, stop using suspect equipment when safe, and get qualified electrical help.

What should I call a breaker that controls nothing I found?

Mark it 'no load identified—do not assume spare.' Expand the ordinary-control check or have a licensed electrician identify it; do not convert uncertainty into a spare label.

Where should the directory be kept?

Keep a legible schedule in the intended panel directory holder without covering warnings, plus a dated copy in the household emergency file. Follow panel-maker and local requirements.
