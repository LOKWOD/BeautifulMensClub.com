Apartment Move-In Inspection and Handoff Checklist: Condition, Safety and Proof

A key is not a completed handoff. Walk the unit before boxes erase the baseline, connect every defect to a room and date, separate urgent hazards from ordinary work, and leave with one record both sides can understand.

Published October 31, 2026. Independent renter-preparation guidance, not legal advice or a substitute for the lease, local housing code, emergency services, a qualified inspector or tenant counsel. Rights, deadlines and required forms vary by jurisdiction and housing program.

The useful answer

  • Read the signed lease, addenda, inventory form, utility allocation, lead disclosure and move-in deadline before the walkthrough.
  • Record each room in a repeatable order with wide context, a close view, plain description, date and the person who observed it.
  • Treat gas odor, active fire or electrical danger, major water entry and missing required life-safety protection as escalation issues—not decorating notes.
  • Send one written exception list through the lease-approved channel, request acknowledgment and assign an owner and target date to every open item.
  • Preserve the original files, submitted form, delivery proof, replies, repairs, meter readings and key count through move-out and deposit closure.

Open the lease before the front door

Build the inspection around the agreement you actually signed. Record the legal unit address, lease start, possession time, property contact, emergency maintenance route, utilities supplied by each party, appliances included, parking or storage spaces, key and access-device count, and the deadline for returning a condition form. A generic checklist cannot change those terms. If an addendum conflicts with a verbal promise, capture the promise in writing and ask for the conflict to be resolved before relying on it.

Create a folder with the executed lease, addenda, listing, floor plan, payment receipts, insurance evidence, required disclosures and the blank inspection form. For most pre-1978 housing, federal rules require known lead information, available reports, a lead warning statement and the EPA pamphlet before the lease is signed. That disclosure is not a declaration that the unit is hazard-free. Keep the exact documents delivered and ask qualified local help about missing or disputed requirements.

  • Identity: exact unit, parking, storage, mailbox and every included appliance.
  • Timing: possession time, inspection deadline, repair window and rent start.
  • Channels: ordinary maintenance, emergency maintenance and formal notice address.
  • Proof: signed lease, disclosures, payments, insurance, keys and condition form.

Capture a baseline another person can follow

Start at the entry and move clockwise through every room, closet, cabinet, fixture and included exterior area. Say the room and surface before making a photograph or video. Take a wide image for location, then a close image for detail with useful scale. Describe what is observable—three-inch scrape on the lower bedroom door, damp area below kitchen sink, right burner does not ignite—not a guessed cause or accusation. Keep original files with metadata and do not replace them with compressed social-message copies.

HUD move-in formats model a sound handoff: unit identity, resident and inspector, date, condition of appliances and fixtures, unfinished work, instructions and signatures. A signature should confirm what the form actually shows, not waive an unresolved disagreement. If management will not attend, complete the resident record, submit it through the specified channel, retain delivery proof and state clearly which entries require response. Do not alter an accepted form later; append dated corrections instead.

  • Room sequence: entry, walls, ceiling, floor, windows, doors, fixtures, storage and outlets.
  • File sequence: room number, surface, wide view, close view and plain observation.
  • Function sequence: control used, expected response, observed response and stop condition.
  • Handoff sequence: submitted copy, delivery proof, acknowledgment, owner and target date.

Separate emergency, safety, function and appearance

Use four lanes so a cosmetic scratch does not bury a dangerous condition. Emergency observations include fire, suspected gas, active arcing, major uncontrolled water, structural movement or another immediate threat; leave the area and use emergency or utility procedures when warranted. Safety observations include missing or nonfunctioning alarms, blocked exits, exposed wiring, unstable guards, broken locks or serious trip hazards. Function covers heat, water, drainage, windows, doors, ventilation and supplied appliances. Appearance covers stains, chips, holes and finish wear.

Test only what a resident can test without disassembly, special tools, flame, a live-load experiment or defeating a safety device. Use the alarm test button rather than smoke; do not open an electrical panel, move a gas appliance or dismantle a detector. USFA recommends working smoke alarms in sleeping rooms, outside sleeping areas and on every level, with accommodations for people who need visual or vibrating alerts. Local rules and the lease determine responsibility, but a missing protection still belongs on the immediate written record.

Check water, air, utilities and the first meter readings

Run each faucet, observe drainage, flush each toilet once, look below accessible cabinets and note active leaks, staining, corrosion, odor or soft material without declaring the cause. Confirm hot water only at a safe fixture and do not adjust a heater. Note ventilation controls, supply and return openings, condensation, visible moisture damage and persistent odors. EPA advises apartment residents to report water and indoor-air concerns promptly because some sources can be addressed by residents while building systems and structural moisture require the owner or manager.

Photograph resident-accessible electric, gas or water readings when the lease assigns the account and access is safe. Record thermostat condition, refrigerator temperature setting, appliance model identifiers, internet demarcation, breaker labels visible without opening covers and any utility account confirmation. A baseline helps distinguish move-in condition from later use, but it is not an energy, mold, electrical or mechanical inspection. For suspected contamination, repeated moisture, combustion concerns or unexplained symptoms, stop guessing and seek the appropriate qualified professional or agency.

Turn defects into a controlled repair handoff

Combine duplicates into one issue log. Give each item an ID, room, observed condition, urgency lane, supporting file names, date reported, requested outcome, responsible party, target date and status. State what access has been authorized and who must be present. Do not repair, discard or paint over a disputed condition merely to make move-in easier unless emergency protection or the written agreement requires a specific action. Preserve replaced-part and completion records when they matter to safety or future responsibility.

Send the list through the lease-approved method and ask for written acknowledgment. If a repair changes the unit, record before, during when appropriate, and after. Test the repaired function without exceeding ordinary resident use, then close the item with the date and remaining limitation. If management does not respond, use the jurisdiction-specific escalation path rather than inventing a rent withholding, repair-and-deduct or lease-termination right; those remedies and notice rules vary and can carry consequences.

Close move-in without losing the evidence

Reconcile every physical and digital access item: unit keys, building key, mailbox key, fob, parking device, storage key, door code and portal account. Record the delivered quantity and whom to contact for a lost or failed device. Update emergency contacts, test the maintenance route and save the building’s fire and evacuation information. Photograph the empty unit before furniture covers walls and floors, then add only a short furniture-placement supplement where a previously hidden condition becomes visible.

Store one read-only copy of the original evidence and one working copy of the issue log. Keep the signed inspection, submission receipt, management response, completed repairs, utility baselines and access inventory with the lease. Review open items after the promised dates and again before move-out planning. The goal is not a dramatic dossier. It is a fair, time-stamped record that helps maintenance act, protects both sides from memory disputes and makes the later handoff cleaner.

Frequently asked questions

Should every scratch be photographed at apartment move-in?

Record preexisting conditions that could be mistaken for damage, affect use or support a repair request. Use a wide context image and a close detail rather than hundreds of unlabeled duplicates.

What should be tested before moving boxes in?

Within ordinary safe use, check access devices, locks, windows, water, drainage, supplied appliances, ventilation controls and alarm test buttons. Do not dismantle, bypass or live-load building systems.

What if the landlord will not sign the condition form?

Follow the lease and local rules, submit your completed record through the approved channel, preserve delivery proof and seek local tenant or legal guidance if acknowledgment is disputed.

Does a move-in checklist prove an apartment is safe?

No. It records observations and handoff status. It does not replace required inspections, code enforcement, emergency response or qualified assessment of structural, electrical, gas, moisture, lead or other hazards.
