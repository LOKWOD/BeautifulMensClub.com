Annual Credit Report Review and Error Dispute File: Compare, Document and Follow Through

A credit-report review is not a score check and a dispute is not a general complaint. Pull the actual files from the authorized source, compare the same account across bureaus, isolate each factual error, attach the evidence that fits, send a precise correction request, and keep the case open until every response and updated report agrees with the outcome.

Published November 9, 2026. General U.S. consumer organization, not legal, credit-repair, lending, tax or financial advice. Rights, addresses, procedures and deadlines can change. Use current CFPB, FTC, AnnualCreditReport.com, reporting-company and furnisher instructions for the exact file and state.

The useful answer

  • Use AnnualCreditReport.com, the federally authorized site, and save each report with its bureau, request date and report or confirmation number.
  • Compare identity, account ownership, status, balance, payment history, dates, collections, inquiries and public-record information across all three files; absence from one bureau is not automatically an error.
  • Give every disputed item its own row, exact requested correction and supporting-document index instead of sending a vague everything-is-wrong complaint.
  • CFPB guidance says to dispute with the credit reporting company and the company that furnished the information; keep copies and proof of delivery or submission.
  • Treat identity theft, mixed files, debt-collection problems and a creditor billing disagreement as related but distinct workflows with different evidence and reporting routes.

Retrieve the reports without buying a monitoring subscription

Start at AnnualCreditReport.com, call 877-322-8228, or use the official mail form. CFPB identifies that site as the free-report source authorized by federal law and says online reports from each nationwide reporting company are currently available once a week. Do not follow an advertisement, search result, email link or look-alike domain that requires a credit card, trial or cancellation. Type or use the saved official address and work from a private connection and device you control.

Request Equifax, Experian and TransUnion deliberately. The three files may use different layouts, identifiers and update dates, so save each as a separate PDF or printed record. Name it with the bureau and request date, record the confirmation number, and store it with the privacy expected for Social Security numbers, birth dates, addresses, account numbers and dispute evidence. Checking your own report through the authorized site is described as a soft inquiry and does not affect credit scores.

  • Bureau, request route and date: ________
  • Report or confirmation number: ________
  • Secure file name and storage location: ________
  • All three received / one unavailable / authentication problem: ________

Build a three-report reconciliation table

Create one row for each identity field, account, collection, inquiry and public-record item. Across the next three columns, copy only the facts shown by each bureau: creditor or collector name, partial account number, responsibility, opened date, status, balance, credit limit or original amount, payment history, last-reported date and remarks. Add a source-document column and a next-action column. This turns three long reports into one comparison without pretending every difference is wrong.

A creditor may report to one bureau and not another, use a different abbreviated name, or update on a different day. Mark those as differences until evidence shows an inaccuracy. Flag facts that cannot all be true: an account not yours, a wrong responsibility type, a duplicated collection, a balance inconsistent with the furnisher's current statement, an impossible date sequence, a payment shown late despite reliable proof, or personal information that suggests a mixed file.

  • One line per identity field, account, collection, inquiry or record
  • Copy dates and status exactly; do not translate a code from memory
  • Difference / possible error / confirmed error / identity-theft indicator
  • Evidence available, missing or requested

Classify the problem before choosing the route

Separate a credit-reporting error from a disagreement with the underlying transaction. If a credit-card statement itself contains a billing error, the site's billing-error dispute file covers that separate process. If the account or inquiry is not yours, use IdentityTheft.gov and the identity-theft recovery workflow as well as the reporting-company instructions. If a collector is contacting you, preserve the collector's information and use the debt-collection documentation path without admitting a debt merely to make the call end.

A wrong current address, misspelled name or former employer may look minor, but identity fields can help reveal a mixed file. A fraud alert and a security freeze are different tools, and neither corrects the inaccurate tradeline by itself. Record what result you need: delete information that is not yours, correct a date, update ownership, change a balance, remove a duplicate, or investigate an identity-theft block. Do not pay a credit-repair company to make a dispute you can state accurately yourself.

  • Reporting error / billing dispute / identity theft / collector contact / unresolved
  • Exact field and why it is inaccurate: ________
  • Requested correction or deletion: ________
  • Parallel safety action, if any: freeze / fraud alert / account contact / none

Build an evidence index, not a document dump

For each item, list the report page, bureau, creditor or furnisher, partial account number and supporting documents. Useful evidence may include statements, canceled checks, settlement or payoff letters, court records, identity-theft reports, correspondence, account-opening records or the creditor's own transaction history. Send copies, not irreplaceable originals. Redact unrelated account details when the current instructions allow it, while leaving enough information to match the item and authenticate the document.

Number each attachment and cite it in the dispute narrative: Exhibit 1 is the highlighted report page; Exhibit 2 is the statement for the same account and date; Exhibit 3 is the furnisher letter. Do not attach unrelated tax returns, full bank statements or identity documents because a generic template suggested them. Follow the receiving company's current identity and document requirements and use a secure channel. Keep the complete sent version and a transmission receipt.

  • Item ID and report page: ________
  • Exhibit number, date and what it proves: ________
  • Sensitive fields reviewed and minimized: yes / no
  • Sent copy and delivery proof stored: ________

Dispute with the reporting company and the furnisher

CFPB advises disputing inaccurate information with the credit reporting company and with the company that supplied it. For the bureau dispute, identify yourself as required, name one item precisely, explain the factual error, state the requested correction, include the highlighted report section and copies of supporting documents, and use the current online, mail or phone route shown by that bureau. A focused written record is easier to audit than a long emotional narrative.

Send a corresponding direct dispute to the furnisher using the address on the report or the furnisher's current designated dispute address. Keep the two submissions separate in the log because their acknowledgments, evidence requests and results can differ. CFPB notes that furnishers generally must investigate and respond within 30 days, but the exact timing can depend on the facts and governing rule. Record the actual dates and notices rather than promising yourself a universal deadline.

  • Bureau dispute route, date and confirmation: ________
  • Furnisher dispute address or portal, date and proof: ________
  • One-sentence error and requested result: ________
  • Expected response window from current notice: ________

Track every response and escalation decision

Log acknowledgments, requests for more information, investigation results, updated reports and furnisher letters. Match each response to the item ID. If a bureau says the dispute is frivolous or irrelevant, preserve the notice and identify what information it says was missing; CFPB explains that the company must tell you why it reached that determination. Do not resend the same packet repeatedly without addressing the stated gap.

If the result does not resolve a documented error, compare the response with the evidence and current CFPB complaint instructions. Consider a consumer-law attorney or qualified nonprofit counselor when the issue is consequential, repeated, mixed-file related or legally complex. A short consumer statement may be available when a furnisher maintains that its information is accurate, but it does not correct the underlying record. Use it only after understanding how it will appear.

  • Response date, sender and item ID: ________
  • Corrected / deleted / verified / more information / rejected
  • Updated report requested and compared: ________
  • Complaint, counsel or statement decision with reason: ________

Close the file only after the reports show the outcome

A confirmation email is not the finish line. Pull or accept the updated report supplied through the process and compare the exact field, status, balance and dates against the promised result. Check whether a corrected furnisher says it notified every reporting company to which it supplied the inaccurate information. Revisit the other two reports when the same error could appear there. Keep the original, dispute packet, delivery proof, responses and corrected report together.

Close each item with the final result, date and retained evidence. Then set a sensible review trigger: before an important credit application, after identity theft, after an adverse-action notice, or during a periodic household review. CFPB says an adverse-action notice can create a right to an additional free report from the named company when requested within the applicable period. Review facts, not scores alone, and never publish or casually email the reconciliation table.

  • Corrected report compared with requested result: ________
  • Other bureaus and downstream records checked: ________
  • Final outcome and closure date: ________
  • Next review trigger and secure retention decision: ________

Frequently asked questions

Does checking my own credit report lower my score?

No. AnnualCreditReport.com states that checking your own report through the authorized site is a soft inquiry and does not affect credit scores.

Should I dispute an error with the bureau or the creditor?

CFPB recommends disputing inaccurate information with both the credit reporting company and the company that furnished the information, using a precise explanation and supporting copies.

Is a difference between the three reports automatically an error?

No. Companies may report to different bureaus or update at different times. Compare the difference with reliable account evidence before calling it inaccurate.

What if the account is not mine?

Treat that as a possible identity-theft or mixed-file issue. Use IdentityTheft.gov, consider freezes or fraud alerts, preserve evidence and follow the current bureau and furnisher routes; seek qualified help when needed.
